# Chemistry Relations 06 — Concentration as Amount per Volume

This document teaches one reusable density-like substrate and then reuses it in solution chemistry.

The point is not only to store a formula, but to preserve the reusable semantic relation between distributed amount and containing extent.

## 1. Reusable substrate

When an intensity or density is formed by dividing total amount by containing size, the reusable family is amount-per-container.

The reusable formula is:

$$ D = \frac{Q}{S} $$

Where:

* input: `Q`
* input: `S`
* output: `D`

This family should be reused when a later law says that an amount is distributed across a volume, area, time, or other container-like extent.

## 2. Molar concentration

Chemistry uses this family in molarity.

The reusable formula is:

$$ C = \frac{n}{V} $$

Where:

* input: `n`
* input: `V`
* output: `C`

This relation reuses the earlier amount-per-container family with chemistry-specific quantities.

## 3. Solute amount from concentration and volume

The multiplicative form is:

$$ n = C * V $$

Where:

* input: `C`
* input: `V`
* output: `n`

This relation reuses the same semantic structure from the opposite direction.

## 4. Connection to earlier lessons

Now chemistry can move across several layers:

* mass to moles by molar mass
* moles across substances by stoichiometric ratio
* moles to concentration by volume division

This makes solutions part of the same reusable conversion network.

## 5. One-sentence summary

Amount-per-container is the broader reusable substrate; molar concentration is a narrower determinate chemistry law that expresses amount of solute per unit volume of solution.
