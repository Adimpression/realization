# Chemistry Relations 10 — Equilibrium Constant as Product-to-Reactant Power Ratio

This document teaches one reusable weighted-ratio substrate and then reuses it in chemical equilibrium.

The point is not only to store the equilibrium expression, but to preserve the reusable semantic relation between multiplied factors, exponent weighting, and reactant-product comparison.

## 1. Reusable substrate

When a state measure is formed as one weighted product divided by another weighted product, the reusable family is weighted product ratio.

The reusable formula is:

$$ R = \frac{\prod_i a_i^{\alpha_i}}{\prod_j b_j^{\beta_j}} $$

Where:

* input: `a_i`
* input: `\alpha_i`
* input: `b_j`
* input: `\beta_j`
* output: `R`

This family should be reused when a later law says that several factors contribute multiplicatively with exponents and are compared by ratio.

## 2. Equilibrium constant expression

For a reaction with products and reactants, chemistry uses:

$$ K = \frac{\prod (\text{product concentration})^{\nu}}{\prod (\text{reactant concentration})^{\nu}} $$

Where:

* input: product concentrations
* input: reactant concentrations
* input: stoichiometric coefficients `\nu`
* output: `K`

This relation reuses the earlier weighted-product-ratio family with chemistry-specific quantities.

## 3. Example schematic form

For

$$ aA + bB \rightleftharpoons cC + dD $$

the equilibrium law is:

$$ K = \frac{[C]^c [D]^d}{[A]^a [B]^b} $$

Where:

* input: `[A]`
* input: `[B]`
* input: `[C]`
* input: `[D]`
* input: `a, b, c, d`
* output: `K`

## 4. Connection to earlier lessons

This law reuses several earlier semantic parts:

* concentration from solution chemistry
* coefficients from balanced equations
* multiplicative combination and ratio structures from earlier reusable families

So equilibrium is more advanced not because it abandons previous lessons, but because it composes them.

## 5. One-sentence summary

Weighted product ratio is the broader reusable substrate; the equilibrium constant expression is a narrower determinate chemistry law that compares product and reactant concentration structures using stoichiometric exponents.
