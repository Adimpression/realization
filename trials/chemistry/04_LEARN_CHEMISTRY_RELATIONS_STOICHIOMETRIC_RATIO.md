# Chemistry Relations 04 — Stoichiometric Ratio from Balanced Equations

This document teaches one reusable proportional-mapping substrate and then reuses it in reaction calculations.

The point is not only to store coefficients, but to preserve the reusable semantic relation between paired amounts fixed by structure.

## 1. Reusable substrate

When two quantities must vary in a fixed proportion, the reusable family is proportional mapping.

The reusable formula is:

$$ \frac{x}{y} = k $$

Where:

* input: `x`
* input: `y`
* output: `k`

This family should be reused when a later law says that two quantities are linked by a fixed ratio.

## 2. Mole ratio from coefficients

In a balanced chemical equation, mole amounts follow the coefficient ratio.

The reusable formula is:

$$ \frac{n_A}{n_B} = \frac{\nu_A}{\nu_B} $$

Where:

* input: `n_A`
* input: `n_B`
* input: `\nu_A`
* input: `\nu_B`

This relation reuses the earlier proportional-mapping family with chemistry-specific quantities.

## 3. Solving for an unknown amount

If one amount is known, another can be obtained by ratio transfer.

The reusable formula is:

$$ n_B = n_A * \frac{\nu_B}{\nu_A} $$

Where:

* input: `n_A`
* input: `\nu_A`
* input: `\nu_B`
* output: `n_B`

This relation turns the ratio family into a direct calculation rule.

## 4. Connection to earlier lessons

Balanced coefficients matter because atom conservation fixed them.

Once the equation is balanced, molar ratios let one known mole amount determine another.

So conservation gives the structure, and proportion gives the calculation.

## 5. One-sentence summary

Proportional mapping is the broader reusable substrate; stoichiometric ratio is a narrower determinate chemistry law that maps one substance amount to another by balanced coefficients.
