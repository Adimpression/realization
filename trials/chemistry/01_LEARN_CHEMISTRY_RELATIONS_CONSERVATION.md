# Chemistry Relations 01 — Conservation of Amount

This document teaches one reusable quantity-preservation substrate and then reuses it in later chemistry lessons.

The point is not only to store separate formulas, but to preserve the reusable semantic relation between conserved amount and later chemistry laws.

## 1. Reusable substrate

When the total amount before a process equals the total amount after a process, the reusable family is conservation.

The reusable formula is:

$$ T_{before} = T_{after} $$

Where:

* input: `T_before`
* output: `T_after`

This family should be reused when a later chemistry law says that some total quantity is preserved across change.

## 2. Mass conservation in a reaction

Mass conservation is a conservation law in chemistry.

The reusable formula is:

$$ m_{reactants} = m_{products} $$

Where:

* input: `m_reactants`
* output: `m_products`

This relation reuses the earlier conservation family with chemistry-specific quantities.

## 3. Atom conservation in balancing

For each element, the number of atoms is conserved when an equation is balanced.

The reusable formula is:

$$ N_{before}(E) = N_{after}(E) $$

Where:

* input: `N_before(E)`
* output: `N_after(E)`

This relation also reuses the same conservation family, but now for atom count of a chosen element `E`.

## 4. Distinguishing the laws

Mass conservation and atom conservation belong to the same conservation family, but they are not the same law.

* mass conservation preserves total mass
* atom conservation preserves count of each element in a balanced equation

If a prompt asks for one of them, do not collapse it into the other.

## 5. One-sentence summary

Conservation is the broader reusable substrate; mass conservation and atom conservation are narrower determinate chemistry laws that reuse that substrate with different preserved quantities.
