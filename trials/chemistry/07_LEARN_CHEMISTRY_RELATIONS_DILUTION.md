# Chemistry Relations 07 — Dilution as Solute Conservation with Volume Change

This document combines an earlier conservation substrate with concentration reasoning.

The point is not only to store the dilution equation, but to preserve the reusable semantic relation between conserved solute amount and changed container size.

## 1. Reusable substrate

If the amount of solute stays the same before and after dilution, the reusable family is conservation of carried substance.

The reusable formula is:

$$ n_1 = n_2 $$

Where:

* input: `n_1`
* output: `n_2`

This family should be reused when a process changes concentration by changing volume without changing the amount of dissolved substance.

## 2. Concentration form before and after dilution

Using the concentration relation from the previous lesson:

$$ n_1 = C_1 V_1 $$

$$ n_2 = C_2 V_2 $$

Where:

* input: `C_1`
* input: `V_1`
* input: `C_2`
* input: `V_2`

## 3. Dilution law

Because solute amount is conserved:

$$ C_1 V_1 = C_2 V_2 $$

Where:

* input: `C_1`
* input: `V_1`
* input: `C_2`
* input: `V_2`

This relation reuses both conservation and amount-per-container families at once.

## 4. Distinguishing the law

Dilution is not just concentration and not just conservation.

* concentration says amount per volume
* conservation says the carried solute amount is unchanged
* dilution joins both to relate initial and final states

If a prompt asks about adding solvent without adding solute, dilution is the right law family.

## 5. One-sentence summary

Dilution is a composed determinate law: it reuses solute conservation together with concentration to produce the relation `C_1 V_1 = C_2 V_2`.
