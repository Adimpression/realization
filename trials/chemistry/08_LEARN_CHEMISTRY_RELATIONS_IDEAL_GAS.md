# Chemistry Relations 08 — Ideal Gas State Relation

This document teaches one reusable multi-factor state substrate and then reuses it in gas behavior.

The point is not only to store an equation, but to preserve the reusable semantic relation between amount, temperature, volume, and pressure.

## 1. Reusable substrate

When a state variable is determined by a scaled product divided by another extent, the reusable family is scaled product-over-extent.

The reusable formula is:

$$ Y = \frac{a * b * s}{d} $$

Where:

* input: `a`
* input: `b`
* input: `s`
* input: `d`
* output: `Y`

This family should be reused when a later law says that one state quantity grows with some factors and shrinks with another.

## 2. Pressure from amount, temperature, and volume

The ideal gas law can be written as:

$$ P = \frac{nRT}{V} $$

Where:

* input: `n`
* input: `R`
* input: `T`
* input: `V`
* output: `P`

This relation reuses the earlier scaled product-over-extent family with chemistry-specific quantities.

## 3. Standard full form

The standard state relation is:

$$ PV = nRT $$

Where:

* input: `P`
* input: `V`
* input: `n`
* input: `R`
* input: `T`

This is the same law written in a more symmetric form.

## 4. Connection to earlier lessons

The amount `n` here is the same mole quantity introduced earlier.

So the mole concept is not local to counting and stoichiometry; it also enters the state relation for gases.

This is a sign of reusable semantic centrality.

## 5. One-sentence summary

Scaled product-over-extent is the broader reusable substrate; the ideal gas law is a narrower determinate chemistry law that links gas pressure, volume, temperature, and amount.
