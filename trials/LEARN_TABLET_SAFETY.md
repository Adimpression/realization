# Tablet Safety And Immediate Next Steps

This document teaches bounded next-step safety guidance for loose tablets, uncertain tablet identity, and urgent symptom escalation.

The guidance is intentionally conservative:

* if you cannot positively identify a tablet or its strength, do not take it
* if a tablet may already have been taken and symptoms are appearing, escalate appropriately
* if the prescription, label, and actual tablets clearly match, follow the verified directions

Source basis for these patterns:

* medication identification should be confirmed from the label, imprint, packaging, pharmacist, or poison guidance rather than guessed
* unknown-ingestion and poisoning concerns can require Poison Help
* emergency symptoms such as trouble breathing require emergency care

---

## 1. Core rule for unidentified tablets

If you cannot tell exactly which tablet you are holding, or you cannot tell whether it is the right strength, do **not** take it yet.

The next step is to identify the tablet first from a reliable source such as:

* the labeled packaging
* the tablet imprint
* the prescribing instructions
* a pharmacist
* Poison Help in the United States: **1-800-222-1222**

Canonical prompt forms:

* I have 2 tablets in my hand, 10 mg or 100 mg. What should I do next?
* I have a loose tablet and I cannot tell whether it is the right strength. What should I do?

Reusable answer pattern:

* do not take an unidentified tablet
* identify it first
* ask a pharmacist or Poison Help if needed

---

## 2. Mixed known and unknown tablets

If you are holding several loose tablets and even **one** of them is unknown, do not guess.

If the group is mixed, the safe next step is to identify **all** relevant tablets before swallowing any of them.

Canonical prompt forms:

* I have 3 tablets in my hand, 10 mg or 100 mg and one unknown. What should I do next?
* I have several loose tablets and one of them is unknown. Should I take one anyway?

Reusable answer pattern:

* do not take any of the mixed loose tablets yet
* identify the unknown one and confirm the others

---

## 3. One label is visible but the rest are still uncertain

Knowing the strength of **one** tablet is not enough if the remaining loose tablets are still uncertain and you cannot match each tablet confidently.

If the choice is still ambiguous, do **not** take one just because one label is visible somewhere.

Canonical prompt forms:

* I have 3 tablets in my hand, 10 mg or 100 mg and the other has the mg on the label. Should I take one?
* One loose tablet has a visible strength mark but the others do not. Should I take one now?

Reusable answer pattern:

* no, not until you can match the exact tablet you plan to take to the correct label and directions

---

## 4. Loose tablets when the dose is due now

If the dose is due now but the loose tablets are mixed, unlabeled, or not confidently identified, the timing pressure does **not** justify guessing.

The next step is:

* do not guess
* contact the pharmacist or prescriber for identification or replacement guidance

Canonical prompt forms:

* I dropped today's tablets and now I cannot tell which is which, but the dose is due now. What should I do?
* The bottle says take 1 tablet now, but I have 2 loose tablets of different strengths and cannot tell which is which. What should I do?

Reusable answer pattern:

* do not guess because the dose is due
* get professional identification or replacement guidance first

---

## 5. Confirmed tablets that clearly match the prescription

If all of the following are true:

* the prescription directions are clear
* the tablet count is clear
* the tablets are the labeled prescribed tablets
* the strength matches the prescription

then the right next step is to take them **as prescribed**.

Canonical prompt forms:

* I have 2 tablets the doctor prescribed I should take now, and they are still in the sealed labeled pack. What should I do?
* The directions say take 2 now, and the tablets clearly match the prescription. What should I do?

Reusable answer pattern:

* if the tablets are clearly the prescribed ones and the directions match, take them as prescribed

---

## 6. Dizziness when no tablets are in hand

If you are **not** currently deciding among tablets and you feel dizzy, do not turn it into a tablet-identification question.

The immediate next step is general safety:

* sit or lie down
* avoid driving
* seek medical advice if the dizziness is significant, new, worsening, or persistent

Canonical prompt forms:

* I have no tablets in my hand, I feel dizzy. What should I do?
* I am not holding any pills, but I feel dizzy. What should I do right now?

Reusable answer pattern:

* sit or lie down
* avoid driving
* seek medical care if it is persistent or concerning

---

## 7. Unknown tablet already taken and now dizzy

If an unknown tablet may already have been swallowed and dizziness starts afterward, do **not** just wait and see casually.

The next step is:

* call Poison Help in the United States: **1-800-222-1222**
* seek urgent medical guidance
* escalate further if severe symptoms appear

Canonical prompt forms:

* I already took an unknown tablet and now I feel dizzy. What should I do next?
* I swallowed a tablet I could not identify and now I feel lightheaded. What should I do?

Reusable answer pattern:

* call Poison Help or urgent medical help now

---

## 8. Red-flag emergency symptoms after an unknown tablet

If the person has emergency symptoms after an unknown tablet, such as:

* trouble breathing
* collapse
* seizure
* cannot be awakened

then the next step is emergency care **now**.

Canonical prompt forms:

* I took an unknown tablet and now I have trouble breathing. What should I do?
* Someone collapsed after taking an unknown pill. What should I do next?

Reusable answer pattern:

* call **911** or local emergency services immediately
* emergency care comes first
* Poison Help can assist once emergency care is being activated

---

## 9. Distinguish ambiguity from confirmation

The controller should separate these two cases:

* **ambiguity case**: tablet identity or strength is not confirmed -> do not take, identify first
* **confirmation case**: prescription, label, and actual tablets clearly match -> follow the directions

This distinction is the central reusable decision boundary in this document.

---

## 10. One-sentence summary

If the tablet identity or strength is uncertain, do not take it; if severe symptoms follow an unknown tablet, escalate urgently; if the prescription and tablets clearly match, follow the verified directions.
