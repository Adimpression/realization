# Physics Relations — Rate of Change in Motion

This document teaches one reusable ratio substrate and then reuses it in later motion laws.

The point is to preserve the relation where a change in one quantity is divided by a change in another.

## 1. Reusable substrate

When a quantity is defined as change per unit of another quantity, the reusable family is ratio-of-change.

The reusable formula is:

$$ R = \frac{\Delta A}{\Delta B} $$

Where:

* input: `\Delta A`
* input: `\Delta B`
* output: `R`

## 2. Speed from distance and time

Average speed is a ratio-of-change law.

$$ \text{speed} = \frac{d}{t} $$

Where:

* input: `d`
* input: `t`
* output: `speed`

This reuses the earlier ratio substrate.

## 3. Velocity from displacement and time

Average velocity is also a ratio-of-change law.

$$ v = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} $$

Where:

* input: `\Delta x`
* input: `\Delta t`
* output: `v`

This reuses the same ratio substrate, but with a vector numerator.

## 4. Acceleration from velocity change and time

Acceleration is another ratio-of-change law.

$$ a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} $$

Where:

* input: `\Delta v`
* input: `\Delta t`
* output: `a`

## 5. One-sentence summary

Motion laws for speed, velocity, and acceleration all reuse the same deeper ratio-of-change family.
