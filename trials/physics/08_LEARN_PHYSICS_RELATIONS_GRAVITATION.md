# Physics Relations — Gravitation

This document teaches one reusable inverse-separation substrate and then reuses it in gravitation laws.

## 1. Reusable substrate

Some interactions weaken as separation grows, and may depend on an inverse power of distance.

The reusable formula is:

$$ I \propto \frac{1}{r^n} $$

Where:

* input: `r`
* parameter: `n`
* output: `I`

## 2. Newtonian gravitation

The gravitational force between two masses is:

$$ F = G\frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2} $$

Where:

* input: `m_1`
* input: `m_2`
* input: `r`
* constant: `G`
* output: `F`

This reuses multiplicative combination and inverse-square dependence.

## 3. Gravitational field strength

For a mass `M`, the field strength at distance `r` is:

$$ g = G\frac{M}{r^2} $$

This reuses the same inverse-square substrate, but now expresses force-per-unit-mass.

## 4. Reusable substrate under the law

The reusable substrate underlying gravitational force is inverse-square separation.

Multiplicative combination appears because the law multiplies the gravitational constant and the interacting masses.

Inverse-square dependence appears because the force scales with `1 / r^2`, so the interaction weakens as separation grows.

## 5. Distinguishing the laws

* gravitational force uses two masses
* gravitational field strength uses one source mass and one observation location

## 6. One-sentence summary

Gravitation reuses multiplicative combination together with inverse-square separation structure.
