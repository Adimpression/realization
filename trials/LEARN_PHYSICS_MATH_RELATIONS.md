# Physics Math Relations

This document teaches one reusable arithmetic substrate and then reuses it in later physics laws.

The point is not only to store separate formulas, but to preserve the reusable semantic relation between the substrate operation and the later determinate laws.

## 1. Reusable substrate

When one quantity is obtained by multiplying two named input quantities, the reusable family is multiplicative combination.

The reusable formula is:

$$ P = x * y $$

Where:

* input: `x`
* input: `y`
* output: `P`

This family should be reused when a later law says that one quantity equals another quantity times a second quantity.

## 2. Force from mass and acceleration

Force is a multiplicative-combination law in physics.

The reusable formula is:

$$ F = m * a $$

Where:

* input: `m`
* input: `a`
* output: `F`

This relation reuses the earlier multiplicative-combination family with physics-specific quantities.

## 3. Momentum from mass and velocity

Momentum is also a multiplicative-combination law in physics.

The reusable formula is:

$$ p = m * v $$

Where:

* input: `m`
* input: `v`
* output: `p`

This relation also reuses the earlier multiplicative-combination family, but with velocity instead of acceleration.

## 4. Distinguishing the laws

Force and momentum belong to the same multiplicative family, but they are not the same law.

* force combines mass with acceleration
* momentum combines mass with velocity

If a prompt asks for one of them, do not collapse it into the other.

## 5. One-sentence summary

Multiplicative combination is the broader reusable substrate; force and momentum are narrower determinate laws that reuse that substrate with different second factors.
