# Learn Biology: Evolution and Selection Relations

## 1. Variation, inheritance, and differential outcome combine

Evolutionary change can be described through:

**heritable variation + differential reproductive success -> population change over generations**

---

## 2. Population-level reasoning

Evolution concerns changes across generations in populations.

So:

**individual event != population evolutionary change**

---

## 3. Selection relation

If some heritable variants are associated with greater reproductive success in a given environment, those variants may become more common.

So:

**environmental context + heritable difference -> differential propagation**

---

## 4. Reuse of prior lessons

Evolution depends on many earlier relations:
- gene expression links genotype to phenotype
- inheritance transmits variants
- signaling and homeostasis affect survival and function
- cell and organism processes instantiate phenotype in the world

So evolution is a large-scale consequence of prior biological organization under repeated reproduction.

---

## 5. Example

Suppose a population contains heritable variation in tolerance to a harsh condition.
If organisms with one variant leave more offspring on average, the variant may increase in frequency over generations.

---

## 6. Reusable knowledge

This final lesson adds:
- population thinking is crucial in biology
- inherited variation is the substrate of evolutionary change
- environment interacts with phenotype to shape differential success
- large-scale patterns emerge from repeated local relations

The full sequence now connects:
cell structure -> transport -> enzymes -> metabolism -> regulation -> gene expression -> inheritance -> signaling -> evolution
