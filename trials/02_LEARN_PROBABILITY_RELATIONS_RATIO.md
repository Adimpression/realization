# Probability Relations 02 — Ratio Formation

This document introduces a second reusable substrate and reuses the earlier idea of total formation.

The point is to preserve the semantic relation between part, total, and resulting proportion.

## 1. Reusable substrate

When one quantity is obtained by comparing a part to a total, the reusable family is ratio formation.

The reusable formula is:

$$ r = \frac{x}{T} $$

Where:

* input: `x`
* input: `T`
* output: `r`

This family should be reused when a later relation says that some normalized quantity equals a numerator divided by a total basis.

## 2. Probability from favorable count and total count

Probability is a ratio-formation relation.

The reusable formula is:

$$ P(A) = \frac{n(A)}{n(S)} $$

Where:

* input: `n(A)` = favorable outcomes
* input: `n(S)` = total outcomes
* output: `P(A)`

This relation reuses the earlier ratio-formation family with probability-specific quantities.

## 3. Reusing the earlier lesson

The denominator often comes from additive combination.

For example, if the sample space is formed from two disjoint groups:

$$ n(S) = a + b $$

Then probability may reuse both lessons:

$$ P(A) = \frac{n(A)}{a + b} $$

So the total can first be built additively, and then the part can be normalized by ratio.

## 4. Distinguishing the relation

Probability and raw count are not the same kind of quantity.

* count is an absolute amount
* probability is a normalized comparison against the whole

If a prompt asks for likelihood, do not stop at the favorable count; form the ratio.

## 5. One-sentence summary

Ratio formation is a reusable substrate for probability, and it often depends on totals previously formed by additive combination.
