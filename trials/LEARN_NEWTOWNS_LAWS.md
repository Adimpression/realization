# Newton's Laws of Motion

Newton's Laws of Motion describe the relationship between the motion of an object and the forces acting on it. They form the foundation of classical mechanics.

---

## First Law: Law of Inertia

An object remains at rest or in uniform motion in a straight line unless acted upon by an external force.

### Equation

F = 0 ⇒ v = constant

### Explanation

If no net force acts on an object, its velocity does not change. This means it will either stay at rest or continue moving at a constant velocity.

---

## Second Law: Law of Acceleration

The acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the net force acting on it and inversely proportional to its mass.

### Equation

F = m * a

### Explanation

A larger force produces a greater acceleration, while a larger mass results in a smaller acceleration for the same force.

### Example

If a force of 10 N is applied to a mass of 2 kg:

a = F / m = 10 / 2 = 5 m/s²

The object accelerates at 5 meters per second squared.

---

## Third Law: Action and Reaction

For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.

### Equation

F₁₂ = -F₂₁

### Explanation

If object A exerts a force on object B, then object B simultaneously exerts an equal and opposite force on object A.

### Example

When you push against a wall, the wall pushes back against you with equal force.

---

## Summary

- First Law: Objects resist changes in motion.
- Second Law: Force determines acceleration.
- Third Law: Forces occur in pairs.
