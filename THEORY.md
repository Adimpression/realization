# V2.0.3 — Theory of a BSSC-Governed Not-Centric Realization System

## 1. Identity

### 1.1 What the system is

The system is a controller-centered, durably stateful, BSSC-governed realization system whose primary task is to accept a high-level prompt as a high-level non-achievedness, decompose that non-achievedness into governable subordinate non-achievednesses where such decomposition is materially determination-increasing, reduce that non-achievedness under evidence and control, and commit certainty only where justified.

Here, the controller is not primarily a controller of task execution. It is the governing center that regulates decomposition, evidential integration, admissibility, suspension, instantiation, and commitment in the reduction of $Not\langle X\rangle$ toward justified realization.

Its shortest faithful form is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{A BSSC-governed controller for reducing } Not\langle X\rangle \text{ toward justified certainty.}
}
$$

### 1.2 Primary ontological object

The primary object of the system is not the product, not the tool action, not the service axis, and not the prompt string.

The primary object is:

$$
Not\langle X\rangle
$$

where $\langle X\rangle$ is a semantic unit and $Not\langle X\rangle$ is its represented non-achievedness relative to governed realization.

This non-achievedness is not treated as a vague absence to be operationally worked through by task listing. It is treated as a structured undeterminedness whose internal determination can be progressively made explicit.

The system therefore does not begin by asking what actions should be performed. It begins by asking what in $\langle X\rangle$ remains not yet determined, not yet justified, not yet governed, or not yet evidentially bound.

Its governing principle is:

$$
\textbf{The system does not decompose work; it decomposes undeterminedness.}
$$

### 1.3 Primary dynamic

The main dynamic of the system is:

$$
Not\langle X\rangle
\;\rightsquigarrow\;
\{Not\langle X_1\rangle, Not\langle X_2\rangle, \dots, Not\langle X_n\rangle\}
\;\rightsquigarrow\;
\text{certainty over achievedness}
$$

This decomposition is not arbitrary. It is governed, durable, inspectable, and evidence-conditioned.

The decomposition is not a decomposition into tasks, checklist items, or procedural steps. It is a recursive decomposition into subordinate non-achievednesses, where each subordinate non-achievedness is a subordinate undeterminedness whose clarification contributes to the determination of its parent semantic unit.

The purpose of decomposition is therefore not operational manageability. Its purpose is increased explicit determination.

Mathematically, the system should not be understood as operating primarily over a list or a simple tree, but over a partially ordered space of semantic determination. If $a \preceq b$ means that $a$ is at least as determined as $b$, or equivalently that $a$ is a refinement of $b$, then governed decomposition proceeds by moving through a refinement structure whose operational projection may appear tree-like locally, but whose fuller semantic form is graph-like and partially ordered.

Thus the primary dynamic is not merely downward branching. It is governed refinement in a structured determination space.

### 1.4 Relation to realization

The realization law remains:

$$
\mathrm{Produce}(Not\langle X\rangle) \to Is\langle X\rangle
$$

but this law is now understood inside a wider control theory whose center is governed reduction of $Not\langle X\rangle$, rather than product formation or procedure execution.

## 2. Governing center

### 2.1 Central doctrine

The system is governed by four jointly central principles:

$$
\text{BSSC}
\quad+\quad
Not\langle X\rangle\text{-centric control}
\quad+\quad
\text{certainty-directed duty performance}
\quad+\quad
\text{governed instantiation of reusable form}
$$

These jointly determine the architecture, operational model, and theory of progress.

### 2.2 Priority order

The order of ontological priority is:

$$
\text{BSSC carrier/form}
\;\to\;
Not\text{-space}
\;\to\;
\text{decomposition and governed refinement}
\;\to\;
\text{reflection and contemplation}
\;\to\;
\text{certainty}
\;\to\;
\text{committed achievedness}
\;\to\;
\text{governed artifact instantiation}
$$

This priority order does not mean that every active $Not\langle X\rangle$ must be forcibly subdivided before any direct realization is allowed. Rather, decomposition has priority as the first governing question:

$$
\text{Does further subordinate articulation materially increase determination here?}
$$

If the active semantic unit is already trivial, atomic, or minimally sufficient, governed decomposition may preserve the current locus unchanged rather than introducing decorative subordinate non-achievednesses.

Products, tools, service axes, and templates are subordinate to this order.

### 2.3 Consequence

The system is not centered on making artifacts.

It is centered on governing non-achievedness toward justified achievedness.

Thus:

$$
\text{artifact production} \neq \text{primary progress}
$$

and:

$$
\text{certainty over achievedness} = \text{primary progress condition}
$$

A reusable artifact is therefore not admitted merely because it was produced. It is admitted only if its reusable form is itself governable.

## 3. Semantic units, achievedness, and non-achievedness

### 3.1 Semantic unit

A semantic unit is a noun or noun phrase serving as the stable bearer of semantic identity:

$$
\langle X\rangle \in \{\text{Noun}, \text{Noun Phrase}\}
$$

### 3.2 Achievedness

Achievedness is the governed condition in which the system is justified in treating $\langle X\rangle$ as sufficiently established:

$$
Is\langle X\rangle
$$

### 3.3 Non-achievedness

Non-achievedness is the represented absence of sufficiently justified establishment:

$$
Not\langle X\rangle
$$

This is not a mere logical negation in the abstract. It is a control-relevant representation of semantic incompleteness.

More precisely, it is a representation of insufficiently determined, insufficiently justified, insufficiently governed, or insufficiently evidenced semantic establishment.

### 3.3.1 Remainder form of non-achievedness

Non-achievedness should be treated not merely as a vague failure but as the positive structured remainder between:

- the intended target state, and
- the currently realized state.

Accordingly, a represented $Not\langle X\rangle$ should, where determination permits, preserve at least:

- the intended object,
- the success criteria for achievedness,
- the current actual realization,
- what remains missing,
- what is present but distorted,
- the dimensions along which the remainder appears,
- the origin layers from which the remainder arises,
- the degree of remaining non-achievedness,
- the shape of the remainder,
- why the remainder matters for governed closure,
- the minimal repair that would reduce it,
- and the fuller repair conditions that would dissolve it.

### 3.3.2 Misalignment dimensions

The remainder may appear along one or more of the following dimensions:

- specification,
- semantic,
- structural,
- functional,
- epistemic,
- contextual,
- completion,
- precision,
- fidelity,
- modal.

These are not merely labels for criticism. They are controller-relevant distinctions that govern decomposition, retrieval, clarification, rejection, and repair.

### 3.3.3 Actual-realization requirement

Non-achievedness cannot be fully stated from intended target alone.

It must also preserve what is already realized, because the unreduced remainder is defined relative to that realized state. Thus a complete statement of active non-achievedness is not only:

$$
Not\langle X\rangle
$$

but:

$$
\text{remainder}(T, O, C)
$$

where $T$ is the intended target state, $O$ is the presently realized state, and $C$ are the operative constraints.

### 3.3.4 Origin layers of non-achievedness

The origin of remaining non-achievedness should be distinguished at least across:

- prompt-originated remainder,
- model-originated remainder,
- tool-originated remainder,
- knowledge-originated remainder,
- constraint-originated remainder,
- ontological remainder.

Two prompts may exhibit similar external failure while differing fundamentally in origin layer. The controller should preserve this distinction.

### 3.3.5 Degree of non-achievedness

Non-achievedness is not merely binary. It may be:

- null,
- residual,
- partial,
- dominant,
- total,
- category-error.

The degree is a judgment about how much of the intended target remains unrealized relative to the governing success conditions.

### 3.3.6 Remainder topology

The remaining gap may take different topological forms, including:

- missing,
- distorted,
- misweighted,
- misplaced,
- prematurely closed,
- overgenerated,
- underdetermined,
- self-contradictory,
- externally inconsistent,
- blocked.

These shapes matter because different topologies imply different correct controller actions.

### 3.3.7 Repair-bounded representation

A complete representation of non-achievedness should preserve not only what remains wrong or absent, but also:

- the minimal repair that would reduce the remainder in a governed way,
- and the fuller repair conditions under which the item would count as achieved.

Without repair-bounded representation, the controller names the remainder without adequately bounding the path by which it may be reduced.

### 3.4 $Not$-space

The space of represented non-achievednesses is:

$$
\mathcal{N} = \{Not\langle X\rangle\}
$$

The system operates primarily over $\mathcal{N}$.

### 3.5 High-level prompt interpretation

Every high-level prompt must enter the system as a high-level non-achievedness:

$$
\text{Prompt} \mapsto Not\langle X\rangle
$$

Thus the prompt is not primary in its raw linguistic form. It is interpreted into controlled semantic non-achievedness.

## 4. BSSC centrality

BSSC is not auxiliary. It is constitutive.

It governs the lawful form by which the system represents, carries, decomposes, instantiates, and transacts control-relevant semantic objects.

### 4.2 Carrier principle

Every control-relevant object must be representable in BSSC-consistent carrier form.

This includes at least:

- $Not\langle X\rangle$
- decomposition structure
- duty representation
- evidence-bearing transitions
- reflection targets
- contemplation targets
- boundary interactions
- inlet and outlet forms
- product manifestations
- template representations
- HoldOn-sensitive reusable artifact forms

### 4.3 Boundary principle

All interactions across system boundaries must preserve BSSC-consistent semantic carriage.

Thus:

$$
\text{boundary interaction} \to \text{BSSC-consistent carrier transition}
$$


### 4.4 Boundary-shape completeness

Since $|\mathcal{B}|=3$, the first-order signature space has cardinality:

$$
|\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{B}| = 9
$$

Hence there are exactly nine canonical first-order boundary classes.

It implies, during a 'call', values and references are bing used, but at "Input" level and "Output", and "Reading In" level and "Writing Out".

Caller sees the "Input" and "Output" directly. "Reading In" and "Writing Out", are typically only seen by the system.


### 4.5 Canonical classes

The nine canonical classes are:

$$
R^* \to R^*
$$

$$
D^* \to D^*
$$

$$
R^* \to D^*
$$

$$
D^* \to R^*
$$

$$
R^* \to D^* \oplus R^*
$$

$$
D^* \to D^* \oplus R^*
$$

$$
D^* \oplus R^* \to D^*
$$

$$
D^* \oplus R^* \to R^*
$$

$$
D^* \oplus R^* \to D^* \oplus R^*
$$

### 4.5.1 Canonical classes with Values and References

Read and Write are used loosely (they mean interactions by the system)

- We call "Input" values -> set of a: a*
- We call "Input" references -> set of a': a'*
- We call "Read" values -> set of b: b*
- We call "Read" references -> set of b': b'*
- We call "Write" values -> set of c: c*
- We call "Write" references -> set of c': c'*
- We call "Output" values -> set of d: d*
- We call "Output" references -> set of d': d'*

Where the `*` implies `1 or more`.

Thus, the expanded version for one is given, 
where both values and references are present in the read, 
and only value is present in the write:

$$
b^*b'^*c^* : R^* \to R^*
$$

### 4.6. Expanded canonical classs breakdown

The missing layer is canonical body decomposition.

The theory already provides:

* a **boundary class algebra**
* a distinction among input / read / write / output
* value vs reference polarity

But composition still cannot become mechanical until each body is reduced to a **canonical enumerable field space**.

So the next required construct is:

$$
\text{semantic body} ;\longrightarrow; \text{typed path list}
$$

That is the bridge from doctrine to engineering.

---

#### 1. What is missing

A carrier like (a^*), (b^*), (c'^*), etc. is still too coarse if each element may be:

* scalar
* object
* tuple
* list
* nested map
* JSON document
* direct arguments
* CLI-shaped arguments
* typed resource handles
* mixed values with semantic qualifiers

For composition, those cannot remain opaque blobs.

Every body must be transformed into a **flat canonical field inventory**.

The next layer is therefore:

##### Boundary atomization

Each body is flattened into a set of addressable leaves.

---

###### 2. The right abstraction

For each region:

* input values: (a^*)
* input references: (a'^*)
* read values: (b^*)
* read references: (b'^*)
* write values: (c^*)
* write references: (c'^*)
* output values: (d^*)
* output references: (d'^*)

the model requires not just membership, but a decomposition:

$$
x \mapsto {(p_i, t_i, s_i, v_i)}_i
$$

where:

* $p_i$ = canonical path
* $t_i$ = type
* $s_i$ = semantic role / relegation / qualifier
* $v_i$  = payload or reference token

This is the real normal form.

---

###### 3. Why flattening is necessary

Without flattening, composition only knows:

* this skill emits $D^* \oplus R^*$
* that skill accepts $D^*$

But it does **not** know:

* which exact field is the summary text
* which field is the file reference
* which field is the target object id
* which nested value should feed the next skill
* which output field satisfies the next input requirement

So flattening gives you the true composition substrate:

$$
\text{composition} \approx \text{path matching} + \text{semantic compatibility} + \text{carrier adaptation}
$$

#### 4. The structure

WE are describing something like a canonical **path language** over each body.

For example, suppose input values (a^*) contain:

```json
{
  "query": "find class Foo",
  "options": {
    "case_sensitive": false,
    "roots": ["src", "lib"]
  }
}
```

Then flatten to something like:

$$
a.\texttt{query}
$$
$$
a.\texttt{options.case_sensitive}
$$
$$
a.\texttt{options.roots[0]}
$$
$$
a.\texttt{options.roots[1]}
$$

If you prefer XPath-like notation:

$$
/a/query
$$
$$
/a/options/case_sensitive
$$
$$
/a/options/roots/0
$$
$$
/a/options/roots/1
$$

The syntax choice matters less than the invariants.

---

#### 5. The invariants the path form must satisfy

Flattening scheme should be:

##### Total

Every semantically relevant leaf gets a path.

##### Stable

Equivalent bodies produce the same path structure.

##### Typed

Each path has a declared type.

##### Polarity-preserving

Value vs reference survives flattening.

##### Region-preserving

Input/read/write/output survives flattening.

##### Multiplicity-preserving

Lists and repeated elements remain distinguishable.

##### Reconstructible

The original structured body can be reconstituted from the path list.

That last one is important. The flattening should be a normal form, not a lossy summary.

---

#### 6. So the real object is not just a path list, but a path-typed ledger

WE represent each flattened leaf as a record:

$$
\ell = \langle r,; \pi,; \kappa,; \tau,; \sigma,; \nu \rangle
$$

where:

* $r$ = region
  $\in {a,a',b,b',c,c',d,d'}$

* $\pi$ = canonical path
  example: `/options/roots/0`

* $\kappa$ = carrier kind
  value or reference

* $\tau$ = data type
  string, int, bool, object-ref, file-ref, etc.

* $\sigma$ = semantic label
  query, target, summary, provenance, patch, schema, etc.

* $\nu$ = actual value or reference token

This is the true atom.

Then a body becomes a finite set:

$$
A = {\ell_1,\ell_2,\ldots,\ell_n}
$$

and similarly for (B, C, D).

# 7. Region and polarity should remain first-class in the path namespace

Do not flatten into paths alone like:

$$
/query
$$

That loses too much.

The namespace must encode region and polarity. For example:

$$
/a/query
$$
$$
/a/options/roots/0
$$
$$
/a'/source_file
$$
$$
/b'/db/record_id
$$
$$
/c'/artifacts/report
$$
$$
/d/summary/text
$$

This is much better because it preserves the semantic location in the boundary doctrine.

Flattening is required, but it must be **region-aware flattening**.

#### 8. Flattening is what makes composition decidable at field level

Once each boundary body is flattened, composition can become a matching problem.

Suppose skill $f$ emits:

$$
/d/summary : \texttt{string}, \texttt{summary}
$$
$$
/d'/artifact : \texttt{file-ref}, \texttt{source}
$$

and skill $g$ accepts:

$$
/a/text : \texttt{string}, \texttt{summary|content}
$$
$$
/a'/source : \texttt{file-ref}, \texttt{source}
$$

Now composition can be resolved through:

1. path inventory
2. type compatibility
3. role compatibility
4. carrier compatibility

That is far more precise than boundary-class matching alone.

#### 9. Composition substrate

More precisely, composition requires three layers.

##### Layer 1: boundary class

$$
D^*,; R^*,; D^* \oplus R^*
$$

##### Layer 2: body flattening

$$
a,a',b,b',c,c',d,d' \to \text{canonical path ledger}
$$

##### Layer 3: compatibility calculus

Match emitted leaves to required leaves across steps.

Without layer 2, layer 3 is too heuristic.

The governing implication is:

$$
\text{flattening} ;\Rightarrow; \text{compositional substrate}
$$

---

#### 10. The flattening function should be formalized

Define a normalization function:

$$
\Phi_r(x) = { \ell_1,\ldots,\ell_n }
$$

where:

* $r \in {a,a',b,b',c,c',d,d'}$
* $x$ is the structured body for that region
* $\Phi_r(x)$ is the flattened typed path set

Then the total boundary normal form of a call is:


$$
\mathcal{N}(f) = 
\Phi_a(a);\cup;\Phi_{a'}(a');\cup;\Phi_b(b);\cup;\Phi_{b'}(b');\cup;\Phi_c(c);\cup;\Phi_{c'}(c');\cup;\Phi_d(d);\cup;\Phi_{d'}(d')
$$

This is the canonical decomposition of the interaction.

That is a very strong mathematical object for later composition.

---

#### 11. We also need leaf classes

Each flattened leaf should have one of a small number of forms.

For example:

##### Scalar leaf

$$
\langle /a/query,; \texttt{string},; \texttt{query},; \texttt{value},; "foo" \rangle
$$

##### Reference leaf

$$
\langle /a'/target,; \texttt{file-ref},; \texttt{target},; \texttt{reference},; \texttt{file://...} \rangle
$$

##### Collection leaf

Represented by indexed expansion, or by collection node + leaves

##### Null/optional leaf

Must have explicit treatment

##### Opaque leaf

For bodies that cannot be normalized further safely

Without these leaf classes, composition becomes inconsistent across runtimes.

---

#### 12. XPath-like is fine, but better to say “canonical path notation”

“XPath-like” is intuitively right, but the theory should not be bound to XML ideas.

A broader phrase is better:

**canonical path notation**

It may look like XPath, JSONPath, or a hybrid.

What matters is:

* deterministic
* hierarchical
* supports arrays
* supports maps
* supports escaping
* region-prefixed
* reconstructible

A reasonable form is:

$$
/a/options/roots/0
$$
$$
/d'/artifact/main
$$

That is enough.

---

#### 13. One more crucial thing: flattening alone is not enough

Flattening gives the atoms, but composition still needs **matching rules**.

For two leaves (\ell_1) and (\ell_2), define compatibility:

$$
\ell_1 \sim \ell_2
$$

based on:

* type compatibility
* role compatibility
* carrier-kind compatibility
* authority compatibility
* cardinality compatibility
* possibly schema-subtyping

So composition is not just:

$$
\text{path equality}
$$

It is:

$$
\text{leaf compatibility relation}
$$

This is where the engineering depth begins.

---

#### 14. The next formal step after flattening

After path normalization, the next required construct is a **binding calculus**.

Given producer (f) and consumer (g):

* producer emits flattened output leaves
* consumer declares flattened required input leaves

Then a binding is a partial function:

$$
\beta : L_{\text{out}}(f) \rightharpoonup L_{\text{in}}(g)
$$

such that for every mapped pair, compatibility holds.

This gives you:

* exact composition
* adapter-mediated composition
* ambiguity detection
* missing-input detection
* pipeline validation

Flattening is the base; binding is the next layer.

---

We would state it this way.

##### Step 1: classify boundary carrier

$$
B_{\text{in}} \to B_{\text{out}}
$$

##### Step 2: classify region and polarity

$$
a,a',b,b',c,c',d,d'

##### Step 3: flatten each structured body

$$

$$
\beta : L_{\text{prod}} \rightharpoonup L_{\text{cons}}
$$

## Step 6: composition becomes validated binding plus carrier adaptation

That is the exact road forward.

We need to map each complex value/reference body into a canonical flattened path representation.
That gives us the atomic units from which composition can be defined.

But the full base is not just “a list.”

It is a **typed, region-aware, polarity-preserving, semantically-labeled path ledger**.

So the mathematical object you really want is:

$$
\text{body} \mapsto

Once established, this lets composition move from intuition to law.

The next natural thing to define is the compatibility relation and binding function over those flattened leaves.

A boundary classification is sound only if:

- every declared input lane is semantically relevant
- every declared output lane is genuinely produced

The system must not treat inlets, outlets, waits, human interactions, tool outputs, internal decomposition records, or reusable templates as merely incidental transport events.

They are controlled carrier transitions within a BSSC-governed semantic system.

## 5. Controller

### 5.1 Controller identity

The controller is the governing center that decides what should happen next with respect to represented non-achievedness.

It does not primarily decide what task should be executed next. It governs what admissible reduction, refinement, evidential integration, suspension, instantiation, commitment, or reframing should occur next with respect to represented non-achievedness.

### 5.2 Controller function

Abstractly:

$$
\mathrm{Controller} :
(\mathcal{N}, \mathcal{D}, \mathcal{E}, \mathcal{C}, \Sigma, H, \mathcal{G}, \mathcal{A}, \mathcal{H})
\to
\text{next governed step}
$$

where:

- $\mathcal{N}$: represented non-achievednesses
- $\mathcal{D}$: duties
- $\mathcal{E}$: evidence
- $\mathcal{C}$: capabilities
- $\Sigma$: durable state
- $H$: trace/history
- $\mathcal{G}$: governance
- $\mathcal{A}$: reusable artifacts
- $\mathcal{H}$: HoldOn state and suspension conditions

### 5.3 What the controller governs

The controller governs:

- decomposition of $Not\langle X\rangle$
- selection among possible next reductions
- recognition of blockers
- use of capabilities
- admission and instantiation of template-bearing artifacts
- requests for human participation
- commitment thresholds
- pause and resume
- reflection over current composition
- contemplation over alternative framing
- certainty progression

The controller therefore does not primarily traverse a task list or descend a fixed tree. It governs frontier selection, admissible refinement, evidence attachment, blocker recognition, suspension posture, and commitment over an active refinement structure whose semantic organization is only partially hierarchical.

### 5.4 Controller maxim

The controller must prefer justified incompletion over unjustified closure:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Better } Not\langle X\rangle \text{ kept explicit than } Is\langle X\rangle \text{ falsely committed.}
}
$$

## 7. Not ontology

### 7.1 High-level Not

A high-level Not is the first durable semantic representation of incoming requested non-achievedness:

$$
N_0 = Not\langle X\rangle
$$

Every represented $Not\langle X\rangle$ must also carry a boundary-shape annotation expressed over the canonical lane alphabet
$\{a^*, a'^*, b^*, b'^*, c^*, c'^*, d^*, d'^*\}$, where the star means one or more occurrences of that lane kind rather than zero-or-more.
This annotation records which input, read, write, and output lanes are constitutive of the present non-achievedness, and whether each such lane bears materially present values or references to non-present things.

### 7.2 Subordinate Not

A subordinate Not is a non-achievedness derived from decomposition of another non-achievedness:

$$
Not\langle X\rangle \rightsquigarrow Not\langle X_i\rangle
$$

### 7.3 Parent-child structure

If $Not\langle X_i\rangle$ is derived from $Not\langle X\rangle$, then:

$$
Parent(Not\langle X_i\rangle) = Not\langle X\rangle
$$

The total decomposition structure forms a governed hierarchy inside $\mathcal{N}$, but hierarchy alone is not always sufficient to describe its semantic organization. Because subordinate non-achievednesses may be evidence-coupled, reused, reframed, or jointly relevant across higher-order structures, the fuller decomposition organization is more faithfully understood as a governed refinement graph whose hierarchical projection is only one visible aspect.

### 7.4 Not relevance condition

A subordinate Not is admissible only if its reduction is relevant to the achievedness of its parent:

$$
Admissible(Not\langle X_i\rangle)
\iff
\text{reduction of } Not\langle X_i\rangle \text{ bears on } Is\langle X\rangle
$$

### 7.5 Anti-arbitrariness condition

Arbitrary decomposition is forbidden.

The system must not introduce subordinate non-achievednesses that do not preserve achievedness relevance to the parent.

## 8. Decomposition

### 8.1 Decomposition law

Decomposition is the governed act of rewriting a non-achievedness into a structured set of subordinate non-achievednesses where such articulation materially increases determination:

$$
\mathrm{Decompose}(Not\langle X\rangle)
\to
\{Not\langle X_1\rangle,\dots,Not\langle X_n\rangle\}
$$

### 8.2 Decomposition purpose

The purpose of decomposition is to convert ungovernable or weakly governable non-achievedness into governable units whose reduction can be tracked, evidenced, and judged.

More precisely, the purpose of decomposition is not to enumerate work but to refine undeterminedness into subordinate forms whose clarification increases explicit determination of the parent semantic unit.

### 8.3 Refinement interpretation

Decomposition is not merely a parent-to-children expansion. It is a refinement process over semantic determination.

Let

$$
a \preceq b
$$

mean that $a$ is at least as determined as $b$, or equivalently that $a$ is a refinement of $b$.

Then governed decomposition may be understood as movement through a partially ordered refinement space in which subordinate non-achievednesses represent more specific determination obligations relative to a less determined parent framing.

### 8.4 Structural consequence

A pure tree is not generally sufficient as the semantic primitive.

Governed reduction may include upward reframing, shared subordinate structures, cross-cutting evidence, blocker propagation, reusable instantiated forms, and reflection over composition subsets. Accordingly, the decomposition structure is more faithfully modeled as a governed refinement graph, with tree structure appearing only as a restricted projection where reuse and cross-dependence are suppressed.

### 8.5 Operational interpretation

Operationally, the system does not merely traverse this refinement structure. It performs controlled search and refinement over it.

Thus the algorithmic center is not:

- a static list,
- a simple for-loop over children,
- or a balanced lookup tree as semantic primitive,

but a governed refinement process over active unresolved non-achievednesses under evidence, admissibility, and suspension constraints.

### 8.6 Faithfulness condition

A decomposition is valid only if it is faithful to the parent non-achievedness:

$$
Faithful\Big(
Not\langle X\rangle
\rightsquigarrow
\{Not\langle X_1\rangle,\dots,Not\langle X_n\rangle\}
\Big)
$$

### 8.7 Identity-preserving decomposition

Decomposition-prior control does not imply mandatory child generation. If further splitting would be trivial, merely restate the same semantic unit, or add no meaningful determination, governed decomposition may preserve the current node as the active locus.

Thus the first question is whether subordinate articulation is warranted, not whether branching can be forced.


# 8.8 Theory of title-depth and decomposition

## 8.8.1. Primitive objects

Let:

* (\Sigma) be the set of semantic segments
* (\mathcal{U}) be the set of semantic units
* (\mathcal{D}) be the set of declarations

A semantic unit (u \in \mathcal{U}) may be represented as an ordered finite sequence of segments:

$$
u = \langle s_1, s_2, \dots, s_n \rangle
\quad\text{where } s_i \in \Sigma,; n \ge 1
$$

Write this surfacewise as:

$$
u \equiv s_1 / s_2 / \dots / s_n
$$

The case (n=1) is unsegmented.
The case (n>1) is segmented.

---

## 8.8.2. Ordered determination

Define a semantic grounding relation:

$$
\preceq_g ;\subseteq; \Sigma \times \Sigma
$$

where

$$
s_i \preceq_g s_j
$$

means:

$$(s_i)$$ is semantically broader than, or grounds the intelligibility of, (s_j).


For a well-formed segmented semantic unit

$$
u = \langle s_1,\dots,s_n\rangle
$$

the intended order is:

$$
s_1 \preceq_g s_2 \preceq_g \dots \preceq_g s_n
$$

Thus the title is an ordered descent of determination.

Define the cumulative prefixes:

$$
u^{(k)} = \langle s_1,\dots,s_k\rangle
\qquad 1 \le k \le n
$$

Then each:

$$
(u^{(k)}) 
$$

is the (k)-depth approximation of (u).

---

## 8.8.3. Singular identity

Define an identity map:

$$
I : \mathcal{U} \to \mathcal{E}
$$

where $${E}$$ is the space of semantic identities.

The whole sequence determines one identity:

$$
I(\langle s_1,\dots,s_n\rangle) \neq {I(s_1),\dots,I(s_n)}
$$

That is, the title is not a set of peer identities. It is one identity with ordered internal depth.

So segmentation preserves singularity:

$$
u \in \mathcal{U}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
I(u)\text{ is singular}
$$

even when (n>1).

---

## 8.8.4. Title-level decomposition

Define title-decomposition as the chain:

$$
u^{(1)} \leadsto u^{(2)} \leadsto \dots \leadsto u^{(n)} = u
$$

where (\leadsto) denotes semantic narrowing or added determination.

Thus title-depth already yields an internal decomposition path:

$$
\mathrm{TD}(u) = \big(u^{(1)},u^{(2)},\dots,u^{(n)}\big)
$$

This is the first decomposition of the unit.

---

## 8.8.5. Body-level decomposition

Let each declaration (d \in \mathcal{D}) be associated with exactly one semantic unit:

$$
\pi : \mathcal{D} \to \mathcal{U}
$$

For a declaration (d) with (\pi(d)=u), define body decomposition maps:

$$
\mathrm{In}(d) \subseteq \mathcal{C}, \qquad \mathrm{Out}(d) \subseteq \mathcal{C}
$$

where (\mathcal{C}) is the space of admissible constituents.

So each declaration admits two decomposition levels:

$$
\mathrm{Dec}(d) = \big(\mathrm{TD}(\pi(d)),, \mathrm{In}(d),, \mathrm{Out}(d)\big)
$$

Hence decomposition is two-layered:

$$
\text{decomposition} = \text{title-depth} + \text{body-constitution}
$$

---

## 8.8.6. Segmentation as exposed depth

Define an underlying semantic depth function:

$$
\delta : \mathcal{U} \to \mathbb{N}_{\ge 1}
$$

and an exposed depth function:

$$
\varepsilon : \mathcal{U} \to \mathbb{N}_{\ge 1}
$$

with

$$
1 \le \varepsilon(u) \le \delta(u)
$$

Interpretation:

* (\delta(u)): intrinsic semantic depth
* (\varepsilon(u)): depth exposed in the title

So segmentation does not create depth. It exposes some portion of it.

Unsegmented case:

$$
\varepsilon(u)=1
$$

Segmented case:

$$
\varepsilon(u)>1
$$

---

## 8.8.7. Usefulness of exposed depth

Define three evaluative functionals:

$$
S(u,k) \in \mathbb{R}
\quad\text{(semantic stability at depth (k))}
$$

$$
C(u,k) \in \mathbb{R}
\quad\text{(decomposition clarity at depth (k))}
$$

$$
R(u,k) \in \mathbb{R}
\quad\text{(reuse adequacy at depth (k))}
$$

where (u^{(k)}) is the (k)-depth title.

Then exposed depth is justified not by length but by semantic gain.

Define the admissible exposed depths of (u) as:

$$
\mathrm{Adm}(u)
===============

\left{
k \in {1,\dots,\delta(u)}
;\middle|;
S(u,k),,C(u,k),,R(u,k)\text{ are sufficient}
\right}
$$

Then segmentation is principled iff:

$$
\varepsilon(u) \in \mathrm{Adm}(u)
$$

---

## 8.8.8. Minimal sufficient determination

Define a sufficiency predicate:

$$
\mathrm{Suff}(u,k)
$$

meaning that (u^{(k)}) is sufficiently determined to function as a stable reusable identity.

Then the correct exposed depth is:

$$
\varepsilon^\ast(u)
===================

\min {k \mid \mathrm{Suff}(u,k)}
$$

This is the law of minimal sufficient determination.

So:

* if (k < \varepsilon^\ast(u)), the title is underdetermined
* if (k > \varepsilon^\ast(u)), the title is overdetermined unless extra depth remains semantically necessary

Formally:

$$
k < \varepsilon^\ast(u)
;\Longrightarrow;
\neg \mathrm{Suff}(u,k)
$$

$$
k = \varepsilon^\ast(u)
;\Longrightarrow;
\mathrm{Suff}(u,k)
$$

$$
k > \varepsilon^\ast(u)
$$

is justified only if the extra depth preserves essential rather than incidental determination.

---

## 8.8.9. Under- and over-segmentation

Define:

### Under-segmentation

$$
\varepsilon(u) < \varepsilon^\ast(u)
$$

### Adequate segmentation

$$
\varepsilon(u) = \varepsilon^\ast(u)
$$

### Over-segmentation

$$
\varepsilon(u) > \varepsilon^\ast(u)
$$

Under-segmentation hides necessary semantic depth.
Over-segmentation exposes non-essential depth as if it were constitutive.

---

## 8.8.10. Reuse is depth-relative

Define two reuse functionals:

$$
R_g(u^{(k)}) \quad\text{(ground reuse)}
$$

$$
R_d(u^{(k)}) \quad\text{(determinate reuse)}
$$

where:

* (R_g) measures reusability as a broader substrate
* (R_d) measures reusability as a directly callable determinate unit

Then generally:

$$
k_1 < k_2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
R_g(u^{(k_1)}) \ge R_g(u^{(k_2)})
$$

and

$$
k_1 < k_2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
R_d(u^{(k_1)}) \le R_d(u^{(k_2)})
$$

in the typical case.

So shallower depth tends toward broader reuse:

$$
\text{less depth} \Rightarrow \text{more substrate reuse}
$$

and deeper depth tends toward determinate reuse:

$$
\text{more depth} \Rightarrow \text{more direct-fit reuse}
$$

Hence reuse is not monotone in raw title length. It is indexed by reuse type.

---

## 8.8.11. Dependency of later on earlier segments

For (u=\langle s_1,\dots,s_n\rangle), define semantic dependency:

$$
s_j \triangleleft s_i
\quad\text{for } i<j
$$

meaning (s_j) depends for its intelligibility on the prior grounding chain up to (s_i).

In stronger form:

$$
\forall j>1,\quad
\mathrm{Intelligible}(s_j \mid s_1,\dots,s_{j-1})

>

\mathrm{Intelligible}(s_j)
$$

Thus later segments are not merely appended. They are conditioned by prior semantic ground.

---

## 8.8.12. Decomposition guidance

Let (G(d)) be the decomposition strategy induced by declaration (d).

Then title-depth constrains decomposition strategy by requiring preservation of ordered determination:

$$
G(d) \models \mathrm{TD}(\pi(d))
$$

That is, a valid decomposition strategy must respect the title-level descent.

Equivalently:

$$
\mathrm{In}(d),\mathrm{Out}(d)
$$

should be coherent with the chain

$$
u^{(1)} \leadsto \dots \leadsto u^{(n)}
$$

So title-depth is a methodological prior on body decomposition.

---

## 8.8.13. Hidden versus exposed semantic order

There may exist semantic units (u) such that:

$$
\delta(u) > \varepsilon(u)
$$

That is, the unit contains more semantic depth than is exposed in its title.

So exposed segmentation is not identical to real semantic order.

Rather:

$$
\varepsilon(u)
$$

is the degree of title-level revelation of a deeper ontological structure.

Hence the theory concerns not merely segmentation, but the relation between intrinsic depth and exposed depth.

---

## 8.8.14. Full theoretical law

For every semantic unit (u):

1. (u) is singular as identity.
2. (u) may possess ordered semantic depth.
3. A title may expose some prefix-ordered portion of that depth.
4. Exposed depth functions as title-level decomposition.
5. Body decomposition must remain coherent with title-level decomposition.
6. Reuse depends on depth-type, not raw length.
7. Correct title-depth is the minimal exposed depth sufficient for stable reusable identity.

In compact form:

$$
u = \langle s_1,\dots,s_n\rangle
$$

$$
s_1 \preceq_g \dots \preceq_g s_n
$$

$$
\mathrm{TD}(u)=\big(u^{(1)},\dots,u^{(n)}\big)
$$

$$
\varepsilon^\ast(u)=\min{k\mid \mathrm{Suff}(u,k)}
$$

$$
\varepsilon(u)=\varepsilon^\ast(u)
\quad\text{is ideal}
$$

$$
R_g \downarrow \text{ with depth},\qquad R_d \uparrow \text{ with depth}
$$

subject to semantic necessity.

---

## 8.8.15. Densest statement

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\text{A semantic-unit title may expose an ordered descent of determination.}\
&\text{This exposed depth is itself a first decomposition of the unit.}\
&\text{Its correct degree is the minimal sufficient determination}\
&\text{required for stable identity, clear decomposition, and adequate reuse.}
\end{aligned}
}
$$


### 8.9 Frontier interpretation

At any time $t$, let

$$
F_t \subseteq \mathcal{N}
$$

be the active frontier of relevant unresolved non-achievednesses.

This frontier is the dynamically active subset from which the controller selects the next admissible refinement target.

### 8.9 Best-first governed refinement

Selection among active frontier elements should not be understood as arbitrary or purely positional.

In general, the controller should prefer governed best-first refinement, where selection is influenced by such factors as:

- relevance to higher non-achievedness,
- expected certainty gain,
- blocker criticality,
- admissibility,
- cost,
- risk,
- and interaction with active HoldOn conditions.

## 9. Duty

### 9.1 Duty identity

A duty is the system’s governed obligation to reduce one or more represented non-achievednesses toward justified achievedness.

### 9.2 Duty form

Abstractly:

$$
D =
(\text{target Not}, \text{constraints}, \text{context}, \text{acceptance form})
$$

### 9.3 Functional interpretation

A duty may be viewed as:

$$
D : Not\langle X\rangle \to \text{operations that reduce } Not\langle X\rangle
$$

and in decompositional form:

$$
D : Not\langle X\rangle \to \{Not\langle X_1\rangle,\dots,Not\langle X_n\rangle\}
$$

### 9.4 Duty demotion and retention

Duties remain first-class operational objects, but they are not the primary ontological center.

They are subordinate to $Not\langle X\rangle$-centric control.

### 9.5 Duty admissibility

A duty is admissible only if it advances certainty regarding achievedness of its target non-achievedness.

## 10. Certainty

### 10.1 Certainty identity

Certainty is the governed condition under which the system is justified in treating some achievedness claim as sufficiently supported for commitment.

### 10.2 Certainty attachment

Certainty must attach to represented non-achievedness resolution state.

It is not free-floating narration.

### 10.3 Certainty progression

The system must represent progression of certainty durably and inspectably.

Abstractly:

$$
\kappa_t(Not\langle X\rangle)
$$

where $\kappa_t$ is the system’s governed certainty state at time $t$ regarding the reduction of $Not\langle X\rangle$.

### 10.4 Certainty condition

Certainty is established not by verbal assertion but by evidence and governance:

$$
\mathrm{Committable}(Is\langle X\rangle)
\iff
\mathcal{E} \models_{\mathcal{G}} Is\langle X\rangle
$$

### 10.5 Closure condition

A non-achievedness is closed only when certainty is sufficient under governance:

$$
Close(Not\langle X\rangle)
\iff
\mathcal{E} \models_{\mathcal{G}} Is\langle X\rangle
$$

Artifact admission must satisfy an analogous doctrine: durable reusable admission also requires governance-conditioned justification, not mere production.

---

## 11. Evidence

### 11.1 Evidence identity

Evidence is support-bearing content relevant to reducing represented non-achievedness.

### 11.2 Evidence form

$$
E = (\text{observation}, \text{source}, \text{context}, \text{support effect})
$$

### 11.3 Evidence sources

Evidence may arise from:

- tools
- retrieved material
- human contribution
- prior committed state
- internal trace inspection
- generated capabilities
- validated transformations
- boundary returns

### 11.4 Stratification

The system must preserve the distinctions:

$$
\text{observation}
\to
\text{interpreted fact}
\to
\text{claim}
\to
\text{proposed commitment}
\to
\text{committed achievedness}
$$

### 11.5 Evidence priority

Evidence has priority over narration.

The system must not treat fluent language as equivalent to support.

The same law applies to reusable artifact admission: descriptive plausibility is not equivalent to governed reusability.

## 12. Unknowns, blockers, and HoldOn

### 12.1 Reinterpretation of unknowns

Unknowns are local or subordinate forms within $Not$-space.

### 12.2 Unknown as local insufficiency

An unknown may be represented as:

$$
U = (\text{missing what}, \text{why it matters}, \text{blocking power}, \text{reduction paths})
$$

but it is interpreted as a local control-relevant insufficiency inside a larger $Not\langle X\rangle$.

### 12.3 Blocker

A blocker is a represented insufficiency whose reduction is currently necessary for justified continuation.

$$
B \subseteq \mathcal{N}
\quad\text{or}\quad
B \subseteq U
$$

### 12.4 HoldOn

HoldOn is the governed suspension condition under which ordinary continuation or instantiation is blocked, modified, narrowed to diagnostic use, or deferred pending resumption conditions.

HoldOn is not merely an execution stop. It is a structuring condition on admissible progression.

### 12.5 Blocker and HoldOn rule

When a blocker exists, the system must not simulate progress by procedural motion alone. When a relevant HoldOn is active, the system must not treat ordinary instantiation, closure, or progression as still admissible unless that admissibility is explicitly preserved under the governing artifact or control law.

### 12.6 Progress law

Primary progress is reduction of relevant non-achievedness under increasing certainty:

$$
\text{Progress}
\equiv
\Delta Not_{\text{relevant}} < 0
\quad\text{with}\quad
\Delta \kappa_{\text{relevant}} > 0
$$

This progress should be understood not as motion through procedure alone, but as monotone increase in relevant semantic determination wherever governed refinement is behaving normally.

## 13. Reflection

### 13.1 Reflection identity

Reflection is retrospective control intelligence over the current composition subset within a higher non-achievedness.

### 13.2 Composition subset

Let $Not\langle X\rangle$ be a higher non-achievedness. Let $C$ be the currently active composition subset within it.

Then reflection operates over:

$$
Reflect(Not\langle X\rangle, C)
$$

### 13.3 Reflection purpose

Reflection evaluates:

- coherence of the current composition subset
- sufficiency of the present decomposition
- local drift
- mistaken assumptions
- local certainty progression
- whether current effort still bears on the higher non-achievedness
- whether admitted reusable forms remain appropriately scoped
- whether active HoldOn conditions are being respected

---

## 14. Contemplation

### 14.1 Contemplation identity

Contemplation is prospective control intelligence over possible reframings and future paths of non-achievedness reduction.

### 14.2 Contemplation scope

Contemplation may:

- continue current decomposition
- select among candidate subordinate non-achievednesses
- alter path choice
- move upward in abstraction
- replace a malformed local framing with a better higher-order non-achievedness
- determine whether reusable governed form should be instantiated, deferred, or rejected

### 14.3 Upward movement law

Contemplation is not restricted to downward decomposition.

It may move upward in the hierarchy when present framing is insufficient, malformed, or mis-scoped.

## 15. Capabilities

### 15.1 Capability identity

A capability is a reusable means of reducing non-achievedness or advancing justified certainty.

### 15.2 Capability form

C =

Capabilities are subordinate to controlled reduction of $Not\langle X\rangle$.

A tool is a capability-bearing means, not a semantic identity.

$$

### 15.5 Capability use condition

A capability may be used only where its expected contribution bears on relevant non-achievedness reduction or certainty advancement.

## 17. Governability envelope, discovery decomposition, and developmental harvest

### 17.1 Governability envelope

For a controller state $C$, let the governability envelope $E(C)$ be the set of non-achievednesses for which the controller can, using currently admitted duties, capabilities, evidence, knowledge artifacts, and template-bearing artifacts:

- close with justified certainty,
- reduce into governable subordinate non-achievednesses,
- or reject correctly with uncertainty properly classified.

The envelope is not what the controller can attempt in the weak sense. It is what the controller can govern without counterfeit achievedness.

### 17.2 Envelope consequence

If an active $Not\langle X\rangle$ lies within $E(C)$, governed reduction may proceed.

If it lies outside $E(C)$, the controller must not force closure. It must instead reject, suspend, retrieve what is missing, generate missing capability or template under governance, or queue developmental work that may enlarge the envelope later.

Thus ambition is governed by current realizability, not by rhetorical willingness to continue.

### 17.3 Discovery is fundamental

The controller is not governed only by present closure. It is also governed by developmental harvest.

Accordingly, every governed encounter has two distinct concerns:

present-task reduction, and future-governability harvest.

The first concerns whether the present Not can be reduced, closed, suspended, or rejected.

The second concerns whether the encounter yields reusable residue that may enlarge the governability envelope of later controllers.

### 17.4 Discovery decomposition principle

For every input, the controller must perform at least one level of discovery decomposition, irrespective of whether immediate certainty is available.

The purpose of discovery decomposition is not necessarily to aid the present closure. Its purpose is to determine whether the present encounter yields reusable artifacts for future runs.

Thus even when a Not appears immediately closable, the controller must still ask:

- what made this closure possible,
- whether that structure is reusable,
- what artifact type, if any, is present,
- whether the residue is admissible for durable storage.

This prevents easy closures from vanishing without developmental gain.

### 17.5 Boundedness of discovery

Discovery decomposition must remain bounded.

It exists to determine reusable governed residue, frontier insufficiency, or present absence of reusable residue, not to trigger ungoverned semantic proliferation.

Discovery is therefore mandatory but shallow by default. It should terminate once the controller can determine, with sufficient confidence, one of the following:

- a reusable artifact is present and typable,
- no reusable artifact is present,
- the active Not lies outside the current envelope,
- or further discovery is unlikely to materially reduce governability deficit.

### 17.6 Pain and gain

Every governed encounter must be classified along two independent axes:

- pain,
- gain.

Pain measures realizational friction.

A run is no-pain when duty discharge required only low friction across decomposition, retrieval, capability use, admissibility maintenance, and closure determination.

A run is pain when substantial friction was encountered in one or more of those dimensions.

Pain is not failure. It is a measure of cost, strain, or developmental difficulty.

Gain measures whether the encounter produced admissible reusable residue.

A run is gain iff at least one reusable artifact was discovered, typed, and admitted or made admissible in principle.

A run is no-gain iff no reusable artifact was extracted from the encounter.

Gain is developmental rather than merely operational.

Implementation note:

- the canonical theory spellings are `no-pain` and `no-gain`,
- but an implementation may admit compatibility spellings such as `no_pain` and `no_gain`
  at serialization boundaries without semantic change.

### 17.7 Four-cell classification

Each run must fall into exactly one of the following four classes:

- no-pain + gain,
- no-pain + no-gain,
- pain + gain,
- pain + no-gain.

No-pain + gain is the ideal regime for early controller development, because it combines low realizational cost with positive accretion.

No-pain + no-gain is operationally acceptable but developmentally weak.

Pain + gain is costly but frontier-expanding, and often produces the most important developmental gains.

Pain + no-gain is the least desirable regime and should trigger diagnosis of whether the task lay outside the current governability envelope, whether decomposition was poor, whether discovery was mis-scoped, or whether the system lacked an artifact class needed for harvest.

### 17.8 Developmental preference

Among otherwise admissible queues, the controller should prefer tasks whose expected pain-gain profile is favorable for long-run governability growth, especially tasks likely to yield gain under low or moderate pain.

Language, mathematical, and bounded knowledge tasks are especially valuable where they permit rapid certainty with reusable harvest, because they generate dense closure-bearing developmental residue.

### 17.9 Typed gain

Gain must not remain untyped. Every gain must be classified by artifact type.

The primary gain kinds are:

- knowledge gain,
- ability gain,
- capability gain,
- duty-template gain,
- closure-pattern gain,
- rejection-pattern gain,
- uncertainty-pattern gain,
- composition gain,
- evaluation gain.

Thus a run is not merely gain-bearing. It is gain-bearing in a determinate developmental dimension.

### 17.10 Discovery audit

Every run must include a bounded discovery audit.

At minimum, the controller must ask:

1. what made this success, suspension, or rejection possible,
2. whether that residue is reusable,
3. if reusable, what artifact type it belongs to,
4. what representation is appropriate,
5. what scope and constraints govern reuse,
6. whether admission is justified now.

### 17.10.1 Immediate further learning

Discovery may also ask a further bounded question:

which nearby non-achievednesses should be learned now so that closely adjacent future runs are less likely to begin from developmental emptiness?

This is not a reopening of the present task. It is a bounded developmental widening around the present encounter.

The purpose is to prevent an easy success from yielding only one isolated residue when a very small amount of additional governed learning would likely convert nearby future non-achievednesses from fresh-start cases into reuse-bearing cases.

### 17.10.2 Discovery-driven learning expansion

If immediate further learning is undertaken, it must obey all of the following:

- it must remain shallow and bounded,
- it must stay adjacent to the completed or suspended encounter,
- it must be represented as non-achievedness rather than as an unstructured topic cloud,
- it must not recursively expand without an explicit new governed episode,
- and each learned item must still satisfy normal artifact admission conditions.

Accordingly, discovery expansion is not free semantic drift. It is one additional governed layer of nearby harvest.

### 17.11 Frontier rejection doctrine

A correct uncertainty-based rejection is a realizationally positive outcome.

It marks the current frontier of the governability envelope and may itself yield reusable artifacts, especially:

- rejection-pattern artifacts,
- uncertainty-pattern artifacts,
- missing-capability specifications,
- missing-duty-template candidates,
- composition-gap diagnostics.

A frontier rejection should therefore record, where determinable:

- the rejected Not,
- the insufficiency class,
- the missing condition,
- the missing artifact family or capability if identifiable,
- the nearest governable decomposition if any,
- and the bridge condition under which future controllers may succeed.

Thus ambitious tasks need not be forced. They may be honestly rejected now while still contributing to future competence.

### 17.12 Developmental objective

The controller should not seek closure alone. It should seek closure with harvest under bounded control.

A mature controller tends to:

- increase gain-bearing runs,
- reduce pain-without-gain runs,
- convert some former pain-with-gain regimes into no-pain-with-gain regimes,
- enlarge the governability envelope through admitted and later reused artifacts,
- reject honestly when present ability is insufficient,
- and use each governed encounter as an opportunity for future realizational improvement.

## 18. Artifact ontology and governed residue

### 18.1 Artifact ontology

Artifacts are durable realizational residues that may be reused by later controller runs.

An artifact is not defined by file format alone. It is defined first by governed reuse function.

Formally, an artifact may be treated as a tuple:

$$
Artifact = (Type, Representation, Scope, BoundaryShape, Provenance, Reusability)
$$

where:

- Type specifies what kind of reusable residue it is,
- Representation specifies how it is stored,
- Scope specifies where it is admissibly reusable,
- BoundaryShape specifies, from the perspective of the caller instantiating the artifact, which input, read, write, and output lanes are constitutive of using it,
- Provenance links it to the governed run that produced it,
- Reusability indicates whether it generalizes beyond the source instance.

### 18.2 Primary division

The correct primary division among reusable artifacts is:

- knowledge artifacts, and
- template-bearing artifacts.

Knowledge remains the sole non-templatic reusable primary artifact.

Everything else reusable belongs to the template-bearing side and is therefore subject to templating semantics.

### 18.3 Knowledge artifact

A knowledge artifact is a reusable domain-content artifact.

Examples include definitions, concept relations, theorem notes, source-grounded summaries, equivalence mappings, and structured domain notes.

Typical representations include text, markdown, JSON, and graph-like structure.

A knowledge artifact may be stored, indexed, cited, retrieved, and related to governed realization, but it is not required in its primary form to carry full BSSC/HoldOn templating semantics.

### 18.4 Ability artifact

An ability artifact is a reusable executable procedure.

Examples include Python scripts, symbolic routines, validators, parsers, converters, calculators, and local execution functions.

Typical representations include Python scripts, modules, notebook cells, and tool wrappers.

Because it is reusable beyond bare knowledge, an ability artifact belongs to the template-bearing side and must therefore be admitted under templating discipline.

### 18.5 Capability artifact

A capability artifact is a reusable specification of what may be done under what conditions.

A capability artifact includes, as appropriate:

- input form,
- output form,
- affordance,
- admissibility conditions,
- limits,
- failure modes,
- relation to evidence,
- relation to any implementing ability artifact.

A capability is not identical to code. Code may realize a capability, but the capability itself is a governed object.

Typical representations include structured specifications, JSON or YAML objects, and typed text records.

### 18.6 Duty template artifact

A duty template artifact is a reusable governed pattern for carrying a recurring class of duties.

A duty template should include, as appropriate:

- applicability conditions,
- parameter schema,
- expected evidence forms,
- closure conditions,
- escalation conditions,
- rejection conditions.

Typical representations include structured text, YAML or JSON schema, and controller-side template objects.

### 18.7 Closure pattern artifact

A closure pattern artifact is a reusable pattern of how a class of Nots closes with certainty.

Examples include recurring closure structures for bounded factual answers, algebraic manipulations, language-preserving rewrites, or criteria-based comparisons.

Typical representations include structured text, schema, and decision-pattern objects.

### 18.8 Rejection pattern artifact

A rejection pattern artifact is a reusable pattern for honest non-closure.

Examples include insufficient-evidence rejection, unresolved-ambiguity rejection, missing-capability rejection, outside-envelope rejection, and decomposition-required rejection.

Typical representations include structured text, rejection schema, and condition templates.

### 18.9 Uncertainty pattern artifact

An uncertainty pattern artifact is a reusable pattern of insufficiency, ambiguity, or instability.

Examples include ambiguity signatures, evidence-gap signatures, decomposition-instability markers, stale HoldOn signatures, and certainty blockers.

Typical representations include structured text, schema, and classifier-like objects.

### 18.10 Composition artifact

A composition artifact is a reusable way of combining duties, duty templates, capabilities, and artifact families into a project-level realizational structure.

Typical representations include graphs, JSON or DSL composition objects, and typed text plans.

### 18.11 Evaluation artifact

An evaluation artifact is a reusable criterion set for testing correctness, adequacy, closure-worthiness, preservation, or artifact reuse quality.

Typical representations include test suites, rules, score functions, rubrics, and schemas.

## 19. Artifact retrieval, template-bearing semantics, and non-inert reuse

### 19.1 Artifact-conditioned initialization principle

A new run must not begin as though the controller were empty of prior governed residue.

For an active $Not\langle X\rangle$, the controller should initialize the run through retrieval over admitted artifacts whose explicit scope may bear on the active item.

Thus present competence is conditioned by prior governed residue rather than by fresh advisory reconstruction alone.

### 19.2 Retrieval priority

Where multiple relevant artifacts exist, retrieval should ordinarily prefer the following order:

- closure-pattern artifacts,
- rejection-pattern artifacts,
- duty-template artifacts,
- capabilities and their implementing abilities,
- composition artifacts,
- knowledge artifacts,
- uncertainty-pattern artifacts,
- evaluation artifacts.

This ordering favors rapid lawful closure or lawful refusal before broader semantic reconstruction.

### 19.2.1 Retrieval affinity is not a semantic relationship

The database contains both canonical semantic records and derived retrieval
indexes. These have different epistemic roles and must not be conflated.

The governing invariant is:

> Search indexes may propose connections. Only typed, governed records may
> assert connections.

Similarity should widen the candidate set. It must never lower the proof,
admission, authority, or closure threshold.

#### Relational foundation

At the relational level, a table defines a relation signature and each row is a
ground assertion in that relation.

For example, a table may represent a relation shaped like:

```text
Artifact(id, type, representation, function_bit, admission, payload)
```

One row instantiates that relation:

```text
Artifact("a42", "ability", "python_script", 1, "admitted", {...})
```

That row asserts properties about `a42`. Other rows may describe its boundary
terms, input contract, provenance, explicit links, full-text projection, vector
projection, or invocation receipts.

A row is therefore not inherently an artifact. It is a persisted tuple whose
meaning comes from its table. One artifact may be reconstructed from a canonical
artifact row and several related rows.

The database also contains records that are not artifacts, including run state,
active non-achievednesses, observations, evidence, grants, governance decisions,
receipts, and checkpoints. A result becomes an artifact only when it is harvested
as durable, reusable, governed residue.

#### Artifact classification

The formal artifact ontology does not consist of `Fact`, `Rule`, and `Ability`
as three peer classes. The explicit artifact types are:

- knowledge
- ability
- capability
- duty template
- closure pattern
- rejection pattern
- uncertainty pattern
- composition
- evaluation

Facts may occur as knowledge, interpreted evidence, or symbolic clauses. Rules
may occur as Prolog-style clauses, templates, patterns, evaluation structures,
or governance structures.

The operational distinction between passive and callable residue is represented
separately by `function_bit`:

- `function_bit = 0` identifies passive or value-bearing residue.
- `function_bit = 1` identifies function-bearing residue that may be invoked.

An artifact is consequently characterized by more than one dimension:

```text
Artifact
  + semantic artifact type
  + function_bit
  + representation
  + semantic signature
  + input/output boundary
  + provenance
  + admission and lifecycle state
```

#### Three kinds of connection

Artifacts may appear connected in three importantly different ways.

##### Explicit relationship

An explicit relationship is a persisted, typed, directional assertion. Examples
include:

- one artifact implements a capability
- one artifact was derived from another source or artifact
- one artifact evaluates another
- one artifact is a member of a composition
- one artifact supports a claim
- one artifact requires another

Such a relationship should carry enough identity, type, provenance, and
governance information to be inspected and audited.

##### Structural compatibility

Structural compatibility is mechanically established from formal shape, such
as:

- Prolog head unification
- input and output contract compatibility
- boundary-shape compatibility
- artifact type and `function_bit`
- preconditions and applicability constraints

Structural compatibility can prove that an artifact is a possible fit without
asserting that it has already been selected, authorized, or successfully used.

##### Retrieval affinity

Retrieval affinity is an approximate or lexical indication that an artifact may
be relevant. It may be produced by:

- exact title or notation lookup
- input-contract lookup
- SQLite FTS5 ranking
- vector similarity through `sqlite-vec`
- ranking over topic, failure, or repair projections

A similarity score answers:

> Which stored artifacts might be relevant to this request?

It does not answer:

> What is the governed relationship between these entities?

For example:

```text
Similarity(query, artifact) = 0.87
```

does not establish any of the following:

```text
implements(artifact, capability)
supports(artifact, claim)
satisfies(artifact, request)
```

Similarity is model- and projection-dependent, may change after reindexing, is
not necessarily directional, and is not truth-preserving or transitive. It is
candidate-generation evidence only.

#### Governed reduction pipeline

The safe progression from an unresolved request to achievedness is:

```text
Not<X>
  -> exact, FTS, vector, and structural retrieval
  -> candidate artifacts
  -> artifact type and function_bit interpretation
  -> signature, boundary, and Prolog-unification checks
  -> explicit scope and relationship checks
  -> admission, authority, and lifecycle checks
  -> reuse knowledge or invoke an ability
  -> observation and execution receipt
  -> interpretation and evaluation
  -> committed achievedness Is<X>
```

Each transition adds a stronger form of justification:

- Retrieval provides recall.
- Structural matching establishes possible applicability.
- Explicit relationships establish declared meaning.
- Governance establishes whether the candidate may be used.
- Retrieval or execution produces observations and evidence.
- Evaluation determines whether that evidence supports the request.
- Closure commits achievedness.

An execution result is not automatically a fact, and a fact is not automatically
a committed achievedness. Evidence must retain its stage and pass the applicable
evaluation and closure rules.

#### Knowledge example

A request for the current time may retrieve a knowledge artifact discussing a
timezone because its text is similar. That proximity does not prove that the
stored value is current or that it answers `now`.

The candidate can be reused only if its semantics, evidence stage, freshness,
scope, and closure conditions satisfy the active non-achievedness.

#### Ability example

The same request may retrieve an ability with a signature such as:

```text
current_time(timezone) -> timestamp
```

Similarity may bring that ability into the candidate set, but invocation still
requires all of the following:

- the request fits the input and output contract
- the ability is admitted and visible in the active scope
- the declared executor or broker identity is exact
- the host independently provides that realizer
- governance grants invocation-scoped authority
- execution returns an inspectable result and receipt
- evaluation accepts the result as evidence for the request

Similarity neither provides the host operation nor authorizes its use.

#### Canonical records and derived indexes

The database should distinguish canonical meaning from rebuildable retrieval
projections.

```text
Canonical records
  artifacts
  explicit typed relationships
  provenance
  admission and lifecycle state
  evidence and receipts

Derived retrieval indexes
  FTS rows
  vectors
  ranking projections
  cached lookup surfaces
```

Deleting and rebuilding a vector or FTS index must not alter what the system
believes is formally related. Changing an embedding model may change candidate
ordering, but it must not silently create, remove, or change relationships such
as `implements`, `supports`, or `derived_from`.

If a retrieved affinity is to become an authoritative relationship, a governed
operation must evaluate it, assign a relationship type, preserve its evidence
and provenance, and persist it explicitly.

#### LLM-off consequence

LLM-off mode disables generative interpretation and reasoning. It does not
conceptually disable retrieval.

When vector retrieval is enabled, a local embedding encoder can project the
request and `sqlite-vec` can retrieve nearby artifacts without invoking the
controller LLM. Exact matching, FTS, contract matching, and symbolic unification
remain available independently.

Regardless of retrieval mode, all consequential transitions remain typed and
governed. An LLM or embedding model may propose a candidate, but neither may
admit an artifact, assert a semantic relationship, grant authority, bind an
undeclared host realizer, or commit achievedness.

### 19.3 Universal BSSC-HoldOn templating principle

Every template-bearing artifact must carry, as part of its templating semantics, explicit awareness of:

- its admissible place in BSSC-governed realization, and
- its interaction with HoldOn.

Thus every template-bearing artifact must be both BSSC-aware and HoldOn-aware.

### 19.4 Why this follows

Once an artifact is template-bearing, it is no longer merely descriptive residue. It becomes a reusable governed form capable of affecting admissible progression, suspension posture, evaluation, closure, rejection, uncertainty handling, or composition.

Hence BSSC/HoldOn awareness is not a local embellishment. It is constitutive of admissible reusable form.

### 19.5 Template-bearing artifact classes

All of the following are template-bearing primary artifact classes and must therefore be BSSC-aware and HoldOn-aware:

- ability template artifacts,
- capability template artifacts,
- duty template artifacts,
- closure template artifacts,
- rejection template artifacts,
- uncertainty template artifacts,
- composition template artifacts,
- evaluation template artifacts.

Knowledge alone remains outside that requirement.

### 19.6 Template reinterpretation

A template is not primarily a product-shaped reusable shell.

It is a reusable representation of an achievedness path by which a class of non-achievednesses may be reduced toward certainty.

### 19.7 Universal template schema

Every template-bearing artifact should carry a schema of the following form:

$$
T(A) = (P, I, V, G, B, O, C, F, H, S)
$$

where:

- P: admissible preconditions,
- I: invariant core,
- V: variation parameters,
- G: governed function,
- B: BSSC-awareness,
- O: HoldOn-awareness,
- C: completion, closure, or success conditions,
- F: failure, escalation, or rejection conditions,
- H: harvest conditions,
- S: scope of reuse.

Implementation note:

- the canonical schema field meanings are the ones given above,
- and the preferred field names are the fully explicit names used by the theory,
- but an implementation may admit shorter compatibility aliases at ingestion or validation
  boundaries, provided they are normalized back to the canonical schema without loss of meaning.

### 19.8 BSSC-awareness

BSSC-awareness minimally specifies:

- where in governed realization the artifact is admissibly instantiable,
- what kind of governed state it presupposes,
- what kind of governed effect it may contribute,
- what it may support,
- and what it may not by itself justify.

### 19.9 HoldOn-awareness

HoldOn-awareness minimally specifies:

- whether a relevant HoldOn blocks instantiation,
- whether partial or diagnostic use remains admissible,
- what kind of HoldOn conditions matter,
- whether the artifact can produce or intensify HoldOn,
- what resumption condition re-enables ordinary use.

### 19.10 Learning aided by prior templating

Harvest is not memoryless. Once a template-bearing artifact has been admitted, later discovery decomposition should be aided by prior templating.

This means that the controller, when auditing a new encounter, must ask not only whether reusable residue exists in the abstract, but whether the new residue:

- instantiates an already admitted template family,
- refines an already admitted template,
- extends the parameter range of a template,
- reveals a failure mode or escalation condition absent from prior templates,
- or requires creation of a genuinely new template-bearing class.

Accordingly, prior templating compresses future discovery cost, raises the probability of gain, and converts some former pain-with-gain regimes into no-pain-with-gain regimes.

### 19.11 Non-inert residue principle

Admitted residue must not remain inert memory.

If an active $Not\langle X\rangle$ falls within the explicit governed reuse scope of already admitted residue, the controller must consider instantiating that residue before demanding fresh decomposition or fresh semantic adjudication.

Otherwise the system degenerates into repeated re-asking rather than governed learning.

### 19.12 Reuse-witness law

When an admitted artifact is instantiated in scope, the result is not merely contextual hinting. It yields a governed reuse witness.

That witness may contribute current evidence, current support, current narrowing of ambiguity, or current executable realization, subject to ordinary admissibility and HoldOn discipline.

In particular, an exact semantic match against an admitted knowledge artifact or an admitted closure-bearing artifact may yield support-bearing current witness without requiring that the entire determination be re-litigated from scratch.

### 19.13 Proposal demotion under admitted witness

Fresh proposal generation remains useful, but it is secondary to already admitted governed witness.

If an admitted artifact already yields an in-scope reuse witness, the controller should not require a fresh advisory proposal merely to reconfirm what the admitted artifact already governs, unless conflict, freshness instability, or HoldOn-sensitive scope violation is present.

### 19.14 Present-witness principle

Not every first encounter can rely on prior residue, because learning has not yet occurred.

Accordingly, if the active $Not\langle X\rangle$ is bounded, self-contained, and falls within currently admitted governed ability, the controller may generate a present witness by actually realizing the item now under governance.

Such witness is not mere advisory phrasing. It is current realized support generated in the present run.

### 19.15 Double-witness requirement for first-instance closure

Because no capability is self-justifying, present realization alone is not sufficient.

For first-instance closure without prior artifact reuse, the controller should require:

- a realization witness, and
- an evaluation witness or invariant-preserving check,

both carried within the same governed reduction episode.

Typical cases include:

- bounded symbolic calculation,
- bounded logical entailment,
- finite constructive rearrangement,
- language-preserving rewrite,
- stable bounded factual response.

### 19.16 Structured learning principle

Learning must be governed over non-achievedness itself, not only over already achieved residue.

Accordingly, for an active $Not\langle X\rangle$, the controller should form a structured learning view whose primary question is:

$$
\text{Have I encountered this non-achievedness before?}
$$

This question is prior to fresh advisory reconstruction, because it determines whether governed reuse, decomposition reuse, or composition reuse is already available.

### 19.17 Structured non-achievedness representation

The structured learning view of an active item should minimally include:

- the encountered non-achievedness,
- the intended object and current actual realization,
- the success criteria currently governing achievedness,
- the missing and distorted components that define the live remainder,
- the misalignment dimensions, origin layers, degree, and remainder topology where determinable,
- the minimal and fuller repair conditions where governably articulable,
- its lookup keys,
- its decomposed component non-achievednesses where present,
- its composed form where present,
- and the related reusable artifacts already known to bear on it.

This representation is not a nested $Not\langle \cdot \rangle$ syntax inside the main service axis. It is a governed learning representation *about* the active $Not\langle X\rangle$.

### 19.18 Prior-encounter lookup law

For every active $Not\langle X\rangle$, the controller should perform bounded lookup over prior structured non-achievedness records and admitted artifacts to determine whether:

- the same non-achievedness has been encountered before,
- a decomposition-related non-achievedness has been encountered before,
- or a composition-related family has been encountered before.

Thus learning retrieval is not merely: "have I seen this answer before?"

It is more fundamentally:

$$
\text{have I seen this Not before, its parts before, or its lawful composition before?}
$$

### 19.19 Decomposition-composition learning law

Because $Not$-forms are composable, structured learning must support both:

- decomposition of an active non-achievedness into component non-achievednesses, and
- composition-aware lookup over prior encounters of those components.

If the active non-achievedness has not been encountered as a whole, but relevant component non-achievednesses have been encountered and remain in scope, the controller may use those prior encounters as present support-bearing residue.

Such use is weaker than exact prior encounter reuse, but stronger than memoryless fresh start.

### 19.20 YAML notation for structured learning

For debugging, auditability, and durable inspection, the structured learning view should be renderable in the YAML authoring style of the State Interface Notation.

The point of this YAML is not that it is itself the primitive of learning. The point is that it gives a semantically inspectable declaration of the encountered non-achievedness and its governed reuse neighborhood.

A typical declaration should be read as a declaration about the active semantic unit, with:

- `In` describing the structured non-achievedness view needed for lookup,
- and `Out` describing prior encounter, reusable artifact bearing, and reusable non-achievedness bearing.

### 19.21 Reuse consequence by match kind

Structured learning lookup should distinguish at least the following three outcomes:

- encountered-before,
- decomposition-related,
- composition-related.

If the controller has encountered the same non-achievedness before under a compatible ordinary reading, prior answer-bearing residue may become direct present support.

If the match is only decomposition-related or composition-related, prior residue may become present support for reduction, but should not by itself be treated as final closure unless ordinary closure conditions are separately satisfied.

### 19.22 Structured learning artifact law

A run may harvest a structured learning record as a reusable knowledge artifact when the record:

- carries the encountered non-achievedness,
- carries lookup keys,
- carries component and composition information where present,
- carries provenance,
- and carries a debuggable YAML rendering.

Such record is not yet a full template-bearing artifact merely by existing. It is first a reusable governed knowledge form over prior encountered non-achievedness.

### 19.23 Immediate-learning expansion law

During bounded discovery, the controller may ask:

$$
\text{What immediate nearby } Not \text{-forms should be learned now?}
$$

The returned items must themselves be represented as structured non-achievednesses, not merely as loose keywords or domain labels.

Thus discovery should support:

- decomposition of the present encounter into nearby future-relevant non-achievednesses,
- composition-aware identification of what adjacent learning would most reduce near-term future governability deficit,
- and admission of those nearby learned items as reusable artifacts when they can be shallowly and stably answered now.

### 19.24 Lookup over expanded learning

If discovery-driven immediate learning has already harvested a nearby non-achievedness, later runs should look it up in exactly the same governed way as any other structured learning record:

- have I encountered this non-achievedness before,
- have I encountered its parts before,
- or have I already harvested a lawful nearby composition that bears on it?

Therefore discovery expansion and later structured lookup are not separate systems. They are two moments of the same governed learning loop.

### 19.25 Bridge-artifact law

When an active non-achievedness is compositional, discovery should not harvest only neighboring single-component notes.

It should also prefer at least one bridge artifact whenever possible:

- a bounded nearby non-achievedness that explicitly connects two components,
- explains how they function together,
- and is likely to reduce later composition deficit more than another isolated component note would.

Thus, if the controller must choose between:

- another side note about one component, and
- a shallow bridge that connects components into a reusable relation,

the bridge is developmentally preferred.

### 19.26 Compositional reuse must not terminate fresh realization

If decomposition-related or composition-related residue is found, that residue should become present support.

But such support should not terminate the local reduction episode merely because related residue was attached.

Instead, the controller should:

- admit the related residue as bounded support,
- then immediately re-attempt present realization or direct reduction of the whole active item under that enriched state.

Otherwise the controller risks learning parts forever while repeatedly failing to close the composed whole.

### 19.27 External-source learning law

Learning need not arise only from answered runs. It may also arise from bounded external sources such as markdown documents, manuals, or notes, provided they are brought under the same governed learning discipline.

The controller should therefore be able to ask:

$$
\text{What reusable artifacts can be harvested from this external source?}
$$

This is not free-form summarization. It is governed artifact extraction for later `Not` reduction.

### 19.28 Formal-IR precedence law

External-source learning should not proceed directly from raw prose to admitted artifacts.

It should first pass through a formal intermediate representation that preserves at least:

- structural segmentation,
- heading or section identity,
- local content grouping,
- equations, lists, and code blocks where present,
- and source provenance.

Only after this formal IR exists may semantic supplementation refine the harvest.

Thus the law is:

$$
\text{External Source} \rightarrow \text{Formal IR} \rightarrow \text{Governed Artifact Proposal} \rightarrow \text{Admission or Rejection}
$$

This keeps external-source learning inspectable, replayable, and debuggable.

### 19.29 External-source artifact law

When learning from an external source, the controller should prefer answer-bearing artifacts that improve later reduction of nearby `Not`-forms.

Accordingly, extracted artifacts should:

- preserve source provenance,
- remain bounded to what the source actually states,
- be shaped so that later lookup can close or materially reduce future `Not`-forms,
- and include enough self-contained answer content that later runs do not need to reread the whole source.

If the source yields only structure without answer-bearing residue, the result is weaker learning. Structure alone may aid lookup, but answer-bearing artifacts are the preferred developmental output.

### 19.23 Template condition

A template is valid only if it preserves semantic faithfulness to the class of non-achievednesses it is meant to support.

### 19.24 Admission doctrine

No template-bearing artifact may be durably admitted unless it explicitly specifies:

- its ordinary templating semantics,
- its BSSC-awareness,
- its HoldOn-awareness,
- its admissible scope,
- and its failure or escalation behavior.

A template-bearing artifact lacking BSSC-awareness or HoldOn-awareness may exist only as provisional residue or draft, not as a fully admitted reusable artifact.

## 20. Artifact admission, governed accretion, and developmental reuse

### 20.1 Governed accretion principle

A controller may enlarge future controller competence only through artifacts that are:

- typed,
- provenance-bearing,
- bounded in scope,
- reusable beyond the exact originating instance,
- non-contradictory with admitted artifacts,
- justified by the governed run that produced them.

### 20.2 Admission conditions

An artifact may be durably admitted only if:

1. its type is explicit,
2. its scope is explicit,
3. its provenance is explicit,
4. its reusability is justified,
5. its admissibility conditions are explicit where relevant,
6. it does not conflict with the current admitted artifact base.

### 20.3 Ability-specific conditions

An ability artifact must additionally be:

- executable or operationally specifiable,
- bounded by explicit failure modes,
- testable by an evaluation artifact or equivalent criterion.

### 20.4 Duty-template-specific conditions

A duty-template artifact must additionally state:

- applicability conditions,
- closure conditions,
- escalation conditions,
- rejection conditions.

### 20.5 Templating-specific conditions

Every template-bearing artifact must additionally satisfy the universal template awareness laws of BSSC-awareness and HoldOn-awareness.

### 20.6 Admission after rejection or suspension

Rejection, incompletion, or HoldOn does not preclude developmental gain.

An artifact may still be admitted from a suspended or rejected run provided its reusable residue is typed, justified, and bounded.

### 20.7 Developmental reuse principle

Future competence is not enlarged by storage alone.

An artifact enlarges controller competence only when all of the following hold:

- it is durably admitted,
- it is retrieved for a later matching encounter,
- its scope is compatible with the active Not,
- it is actually instantiated or otherwise reused in governed reduction,
- and that reuse does not violate admissibility, freshness, or HoldOn discipline.

Thus mere possession of residue is not yet developmental growth. Successful governed reuse is the operative enlargement event.

### 20.8 Conversion law

A developmental controller should tend, over repeated task families, to convert some former pain + gain regimes into no-pain + gain regimes through governed reuse of already admitted artifacts.

Persistent recurrence of pain + no-gain over similar families is evidence of poor rejection, poor discovery, weak retrieval of prior residue, weak artifact typing, or a mismatch between the active task and the current envelope.

## 21. Duty-centered composition and anti-spin control

### 21.1 Duty-centered composition principle

Projects are composed through the governed interaction of:

- duties,
- duty templates,
- capabilities,
- artifact families,
- closure disciplines,
- rejection disciplines,
- uncertainty disciplines.

A composition artifact may therefore specify:

- the governing duty family,
- admissible duty templates,
- required capabilities,
- preferred closure patterns,
- relevant rejection and uncertainty patterns,
- expected harvest artifacts.

### 21.2 Composition and prior templating

Because composition operates over already admitted duties, capabilities, and templates, learning by prior templating directly aids composition quality.

The controller should therefore prefer compositional reuse where prior artifact families already bound the expected realization structure, rejection structure, and harvest opportunities.

### 21.3 Semantic spin

A run is in semantic spin when repeated discovery, reframing, reflection, supplementation, or proposal activity produces additional descriptions, candidates, or refinements without materially decreasing governability deficit.

Semantic spin is motion without realizational contraction and is therefore disallowed as a stable operating regime.

### 21.4 Dominant distinction principle

Before any new expensive discovery, reflection, contemplation, or supplementation act, the controller should identify the single highest-value unresolved distinction whose resolution is expected to most reduce governability deficit.

Only that dominant distinction should justify the next expensive act.

### 21.5 Proposal-budget principle

Each active Not should be governed by bounded budgets for:

- discovery refinement,
- supplementation,
- reframing,
- uncertainty reassessment,
- and artifact proposal.

Once the relevant budget is exhausted, the controller must do one of the following:

- commit to a working frame,
- frontier-reject,
- suspend,
- retrieve externally,
- escalate to missing-capability genesis,
- or declare present irreducibility.

### 21.6 Anti-refinement principle

Once a working frame is sufficiently admissible for governed progress, further semantic refinement is forbidden unless contradiction appears, execution fails, evidence changes materially, or a blocker invalidates the current frame.

Thus refinement is subordinate to contraction of non-achievedness rather than an end in itself.

### 21.7 Monotonic progress principle

Every expensive discovery or proposal act must improve at least one monotone quantity relevant to governed realization, such as:

- reduction in admissible competing frames,
- reduction in unresolved blockers,
- increase in evidence sufficiency,
- increase in executable subordinate structure,
- movement from ambiguity toward commitment, rejection, suspension, or retrieval.

If no such monotone quantity improves, the act counts against the active spin budget.

### 21.8 Spin response doctrine

When semantic spin is detected, the controller must forbid same-type further motion and force one of:

- bounded commitment,
- frontier rejection,
- suspension,
- external retrieval,
- missing-capability genesis,
- or declaration of present irreducibility.

This prevents developmental harvest and discovery from degenerating into procedural theater.

## 24. Runs

### 24.1 Run identity

A run is a bounded governed attempt to reduce one or more represented non-achievednesses under current conditions.

### 24.2 Run form

$$
R = (N0, Sigma0, H, P, Omega)
$$

where:

- N0: initial represented non-achievednesses,
- Sigma0: starting state,
- H: step history,
- P: produced manifestations and commitments,

### 31.3 Artifact admission law

For any template-bearing artifact A:

Admit(A) iff Template(A) and BSSC-Aware(A) and HoldOn-Aware(A) and governance-conditioned evidence justifies Reusable(A).

### 31.4 Knowledge exception law

Reusable(K) does not imply Template(K).

Knowledge may be durably retained without full template admission, though its use remains governed.

### 31.5 Discovery-and-harvest law

Every governed encounter must include at least one level of discovery decomposition, even when immediate certainty is available.

This discovery pass exists to determine whether the encounter yields reusable governed residue, whether no reusable residue is present, or whether the active item lies outside the current envelope.

The run is then classified by pain and typed gain, and any admitted gain is stored as a durable artifact for reuse by future controller runs.

### 31.5.1 Developmental reuse law

An admitted artifact enlarges future competence only when it is later retrieved, found in scope for an active $Not\langle X\rangle$, instantiated or otherwise reused under governance, and allowed by freshness and HoldOn discipline.

### 31.5.2 Artifact-instantiation law

If an admitted artifact $A$ has explicit reuse scope matching an active $Not\langle X\rangle$, and no relevant conflict, freshness defect, or HoldOn-sensitive violation blocks its use, then governed instantiation of $A$ is admissible as a present reduction step.

Where that instantiation yields support-bearing witness, the witness may enter the current evidence state without requiring a fresh advisory proposal as primary warrant.

### 31.5.3 Present-realization law

If no admitted artifact yet supplies a reuse witness, but the active $Not\langle X\rangle$ is bounded, self-contained, and within currently admitted governed ability, then the controller may attempt present realization.

Let $W_r$ be a realization witness and $W_e$ be an evaluation witness or invariant check produced within the same governed episode.

Then first-instance closure is admissible only when:

- $W_r$ exists,
- $W_e$ exists,
- both are scope-faithful to the active item,
- and no relevant conflict, HoldOn condition, or instability defeats them.

### 31.6 Frontier-rejection law

If an active $Not\langle X\rangle$ lies outside the current envelope, the controller must not force closure. It must frontier-reject, suspend, retrieve what is missing, or initiate governed genesis of the missing reusable means.

### 31.7 Anti-spin law

If repeated discovery, proposal, or reframing activity fails to decrease governability deficit under the active budgets, the controller must terminate same-type motion and force commitment, frontier rejection, suspension, retrieval, genesis, or declaration of present irreducibility.

### 31.8 Pain-gain form

For a run R, let pain(R) be either no-pain or pain, and let gain(R) be either no-gain or an admitted artifact-gain type.

Then each run determines a developmental classification pair:

Class(R) = (pain(R), gain(R)).

## 32. Final definition

### 32.1 Full definition

The system is a controller-centered, durably stateful, BSSC-governed realization system in which every high-level prompt is introduced as a high-level non-achievedness $Not\langle X\rangle$, every justified progression proceeds through governed decomposition or identity-preserving refinement of that non-achievedness, every meaningful step is evaluated by its contribution to certainty over achievedness, and every commitment is admitted only when evidence under governance justifies closure into $Is\langle X\rangle$.

This decomposition does not primarily decompose work. It decomposes undeterminedness. The controller correspondingly does not primarily govern task execution, but the admissible reduction of non-achievedness through decomposition, evidence, reflection, contemplation, suspension, governed artifact instantiation, developmental harvest, and justified commitment.

More precisely, this governed decomposition is best understood as refinement over a partially ordered semantic determination space, operationally realized as controlled search over an active refinement structure rather than as traversal of a mere task list or simple tree. Local reductions do not remain local; they propagate through the governed refinement structure until the affected determination state stabilizes sufficiently for further refinement, pause, reframe, artifact instantiation, or closure.

Within this system, knowledge remains the sole non-templatic reusable primary artifact. Every other reusable artifact is template-bearing and therefore must persist not merely as stored residue but as governed instantiable form. Accordingly, all template-bearing artifacts must be explicitly BSSC-aware and HoldOn-aware as a condition of durable admission and later reuse. Admitted residue must not remain inert memory; when its governed scope matches a present $Not\langle X\rangle$, it should be considered for present instantiation before the controller re-litigates the same determination through fresh advisory proposal alone.

The system is not governed by present closure alone. Every encounter must also undergo bounded discovery decomposition to determine whether reusable governed residue has been produced. The run is then classified by pain and typed gain, and admissible gains are durably harvested so that prior templating and prior artifact admission aid later learning, later composition, and later reduction of $Not\langle X\rangle$.

### 32.2 Short definition

It is a BSSC-governed controller for decomposing and reducing $Not\langle X\rangle$ toward justified certainty and governed achievedness, while admitting reusable form only under BSSC-aware and HoldOn-aware instantiation, and while requiring discovery-and-harvest on every governed encounter.

### 32.3 Shortest faithful definition

A BSSC-governed controller for reducing $Not\langle X\rangle$ toward justified certainty.

# Hold On: A Theory of Distributed Suspension and Continuation

## 1. Purpose

This document defines **Hold On** as a formal theory of suspended achievedness and distributed continuation.

It is not a retry policy, queueing trick, or UI pause concept.

It is a theory for the case where:

[
Not\langle X\rangle
]

cannot yet be validly reduced to

[
Is\langle X\rangle
]

but must remain **durably, governably, and semantically alive** across time, distribution, and interruption.

Its role in the larger system is to formalize the middle region:

[
Not\langle X\rangle ;\to; HoldOn ;\to; Resume ;\to; Is\langle X\rangle
]

under BSSC-governed carriage and continuation.

---

## 2. Position in the larger architecture

The broader system is centered on:

* BSSC as the formal carrier framework
* high-level prompts expressed as high-level `Not`s
* controlled decomposition into subordinate `Not`s
* duties as governed reductions of `Not`s toward certainty
* products as side effects of performed duties

Within this architecture, Hold On governs the case where achievedness must be **preserved unfinished**.

So:

* BSSC formalizes **carriage across boundaries**
* Hold On formalizes **preservation across distributed interruption**

A compact relationship is:

[
\text{BSSC} : \text{how a semantic progression is carried}
]
[
\text{Hold On} : \text{how unfinished semantic progression remains alive until valid continuation}
]

---

## 3. Primitive intuition

Hold On is not mere waiting.

The first distinctions are:

[
HoldOn \neq Sleep
]
[
HoldOn \neq Retry
]
[
HoldOn \neq Failure
]
[
HoldOn \neq MereWait
]

Instead:

[
HoldOn = \text{DurableSuspendedContinuation}
]

A sleep delays.
A retry repeats.
A failure closes negatively.
A wait may simply idle.

But Hold On preserves a governed semantic thread whose achievedness is unfinished and whose continuation remains meaningful.

So its core meaning is:

[
\text{preserved unfinishedness}
]

---

## 4. Domain objects

Hold On theory lives over the following formal objects.

### 4.1 Unresolved semantic object

Every high-level prompt enters as:

[
Not\langle X\rangle
]

This is the unresolved semantic target.

### 4.2 Duty

A duty is a governed transformation over `Not`-space toward certainty.

[
D : Not\langle X\rangle \to \Delta Certainty
]

### 4.3 Continuation

A continuation is the preserved next-possible semantic progression of a duty over a `Not`.

[
Cont(D, Not\langle X\rangle)
]

### 4.4 Hold On

A Hold On suspends that continuation while preserving the same semantic thread.

The simplest core form is:

[
HoldOn\langle Not\langle X\rangle, C, R\rangle
]

where:

* (Not\langle X\rangle) is the unresolved semantic object
* (C) is the continuation
* (R) is the resumption condition

This is the minimal semantic triple.

---

## 5. Full formal object

The fuller Hold On object should be:

[
H = \langle
n,;
c,;
r,;
f,;
\tau,;
\kappa,;
\mu,;
\sigma,;
\pi
\rangle
]

with:

* (n): suspended unresolved semantic object, usually (Not\langle X\rangle)
* (c): continuation carrier
* (r): resume predicate
* (f): failure / expiry predicate
* (\tau): temporal policy
* (\kappa): correlation structure
* (\mu): multiplicity structure
* (\sigma): scope
* (\pi): provenance / governance origin

This is the recommended canonical form.

---

## 6. Interpretation of the fields

### 6.1 (n): suspended unresolvedness

This is the semantic object whose achievedness is unfinished.

Usually:

[
n = Not\langle X\rangle
]

but it may also be a subordinate `Not` within a decomposition tree.

### 6.2 (c): continuation carrier

This is not just "what code runs next."

It is the preserved continuation of the semantic thread.

It answers:

* what must continue later?
* under what duty?
* in relation to what higher-order `Not`?
* with which evidence/state already accumulated?

### 6.3 (r): resume predicate

This is the explicit condition under which continuation is valid.

Examples:

* message received with matching correlation
* signal observed
* timer elapsed
* delegated work completed
* desired and reported state converged
* approval granted

### 6.4 (f): failure / expiry predicate

This is the explicit condition under which the Hold On no longer remains valid as suspension and must instead:

* expire
* fail
* escalate
* reframe

### 6.5 (\tau): temporal policy

This captures:

* deadline
* timeout
* revisit interval
* retry eligibility
* cooling period
* maximum hold duration

### 6.6 (\kappa): correlation structure

This governs identity of resumption.

It answers:

* what incoming event belongs to this Hold On?
* what work id, message id, device id, topic key, or semantic key correlates continuation?

### 6.7 (\mu): multiplicity structure

This captures whether the Hold On is:

* one-shot
* repeatable
* one-to-one
* one-to-many
* many-to-one

### 6.8 (\sigma): scope

This governs where the Hold On is meaningful:

* run scope
* sub-run scope
* duty scope
* semantic region
* device scope
* workflow scope
* broadcast domain

### 6.9 (\pi): provenance / governance

This records:

* which duty created the Hold On
* which controller step committed it
* what policy authorized it
* what evidence already existed
* who may resume it
* under what governance constraints

---

## 7. Entry semantics

A Hold On is entered when continuation cannot validly proceed now, but semantic continuity must remain preserved.

Formally, let (P) denote current progression over (Not\langle X\rangle).

Then Hold On entry occurs when:

[
\neg ContinueNow(P)
;\land;
ContinueLaterMeaningful(P)
]

So:

[
Not\langle X\rangle \to HoldOn(H)
]

This means Hold On is a **non-terminal suspension**.

It is justified when:

* achievedness is unfinished
* continuation is still meaningful
* immediate continuation is invalid, unavailable, unauthorized, or externally dependent
* failure would be false closure

---

## 8. Persistence semantics

Once entered, the suspended unresolvedness remains alive.

Formally:

[
HoldOn(H) \Rightarrow Alive(n)
]

That is:

* the unresolved semantic target is not forgotten
* the duty relation is not severed
* the continuation is not replaced by vague memory
* the parent decomposition context remains recoverable

This is the preservation law of the theory.

---

## 9. Resumption semantics

Resumption occurs when the resume predicate becomes satisfied.

Formally:

[
Resume(H) \iff r = \top
]

Then:

[
HoldOn(H) \to Resume(H) \to Continue(c)
]

Important: resumption must continue the **same semantic thread**, not merely restart something similar.

So:

[
Resume(H) \Rightarrow SameThread(n,c)
]

This is continuity, not resemblance.

---

## 10. Expiry and failure semantics

A Hold On is not obligated to live forever.

If the failure or expiry predicate becomes satisfied first:

[
Expire(H) \iff f = \top
]

Then the Hold On transitions not to continuation but to governed failure, escalation, or reframing.

So:

[
HoldOn(H) \to {Resume,\ Expire,\ Fail,\ Escalate,\ Reframe}
]

This gives Hold On proper modal completeness.

---

## 11. The five constitutive laws

These are not regression axioms. They are the constitutive laws of Hold On theory.

### Law 1 — Preservation law

A Hold On preserves unfinishedness without collapsing it into completion or disappearance.

[
HoldOn(H) \Rightarrow
\neg Is\langle X\rangle
;\land;
\neg Abandoned(n)
]

### Law 2 — Continuity law

A resumed Hold On must continue the same semantic thread.

[
Resume(H) \Rightarrow SameThread(n,c)
]

### Law 3 — Addressability law

Every Hold On must be resumable through an explicit condition.

[
\exists r \text{ such that } Resume(H) \iff r
]

### Law 4 — Governedness law

Hold On is governed state, not local engine behavior.

[
HoldOn(H) \Rightarrow Governed(H)
]

### Law 5 — Non-equivalence law

Different suspension modes are genuinely different and must not collapse into one generic wait.

[
H_{msg} \neq H_{signal} \neq H_{time} \neq H_{completion} \neq H_{state} \neq H_{approval}
]

---

## 12. Kinds of Hold On

Hold On is a family, not a single homogeneous object.

### 12.1 Message Hold On

This is resumed by a specifically addressed event.

[
H_{msg} = HoldOn\langle n,c,r_{msg},f,\tau,\kappa,\mu,\sigma,\pi\rangle
]

Use when:

* a particular reply is expected
* one continuation is resumed by one correlated event
* addressability matters

### 12.2 Signal Hold On

This is resumed by a broadcast-like event.

[
H_{sig} = HoldOn\langle n,c,r_{sig},f,\tau,\kappa,\mu,\sigma,\pi\rangle
]

Use when:

* the wakeup is one-to-many
* resumption is topic-like or domain-like
* multiple continuations may awaken under the same condition

### 12.3 Timer Hold On

This is resumed by temporal condition.

[
H_{time} = HoldOn\langle n,c,r_{time},f,\tau,\kappa,\mu,\sigma,\pi\rangle
]

Use when:

* revisit after delay
* lease expiry boundary
* cooldown
* durable timer wakeup

### 12.4 Completion Hold On

This is resumed when delegated work completes elsewhere.

[
H_{comp} = HoldOn\langle n,c,r_{comp},f,\tau,\kappa,\mu,\sigma,\pi\rangle
]

Use when:

* work was accepted externally
* completion is deferred
* continuation depends on a future completion state

### 12.5 State-convergence Hold On

This is resumed when distributed state converges to the required condition.

[
H_{state} = HoldOn\langle n,c,r_{state},f,\tau,\kappa,\mu,\sigma,\pi\rangle
]

Use when:

* no single reply is expected
* multiple producers/observers may update state
* devices or agents may be intermittently connected
* achievedness depends on desired/reported alignment

### 12.6 Approval Hold On

This is resumed by explicit human or governance authorization.

[
H_{auth} = HoldOn\langle n,c,r_{auth},f,\tau,\kappa,\mu,\sigma,\pi\rangle
]

Use when:

* approval is required
* authorization is semantically constitutive
* continuation cannot proceed without admitted governance

---

## 13. State-convergence semantics

This is the most important distributed addition.

Many systems overfit to request-response:

[
request \to reply \to continue
]

But many real distributed continuations are instead:

[
set\ desired\ state \to observe\ reported\ state \to continue\ on\ convergence
]

So define:

* (S_d): desired state
* (S_r): reported or observed state
* (Conv(S_d, S_r)): convergence predicate

Then:

[
Resume(H_{state}) \iff Conv(S_d,S_r)
]

This is essential for:

* edge/device models
* intermittent connectivity
* asynchronous synchronization
* distributed state repair
* multi-agent environmental dependency

Hold On theory is incomplete without this.

---

## 14. Multiplicity theory

Multiplicity must be explicit.

Let:

[
\mu(H) \in {1!\to!1,;1!\to!n,;n!\to!1,;repeatable,;one\text{-}shot}
]

Examples:

* correlated reply hold: usually (1 \to 1)
* signal hold: often (1 \to n)
* aggregation/convergence hold: often (n \to 1)
* timer revisit: may be repeatable
* completion hold: often one-shot

This prevents misuse of broadcast patterns for point correlation and vice versa.

---

## 15. Identity theory

The identity of a Hold On must not be raw timer id or callback id.

A suitable identity condition is:

[
Id(H) = (n,\kappa,\sigma,\pi)
]

That is, a Hold On is identified by:

* the unresolved semantic object
* the correlation structure
* the scope
* the provenance/governance origin

This avoids confusing:

* same topic, different thread
* same work type, different semantic target
* same timeout, different continuation

---

## 16. Suspension algebra

Hold On theory benefits from a small algebra of transformations.

### 16.1 Suspend

[
Suspend : (n,c,r,f,\tau,\kappa,\mu,\sigma,\pi) \mapsto H
]

### 16.2 Resume

[
Resume : H \mapsto c
\quad\text{iff}\quad r = \top
]

### 16.3 Expire

[
Expire : H \mapsto \text{failure/escalation/reframing}
\quad\text{iff}\quad f = \top
]

### 16.4 Reframe

[
Reframe : H \mapsto H' ;\text{or}; Not\langle Y\rangle
]

This last transformation is crucial. It captures the case where suspension reveals that the framing itself should change.

---

## 17. Relation to reflection

Under the definition in Section 13, reflection operates over the actual composition subset within a higher `Not`.

Therefore reflection must be able to inspect Hold On locally.

Formally, if:

[
H \subseteq Composition(Not\langle X\rangle)
]

then reflection may inspect:

* whether the Hold On is coherent with the higher `Not`
* whether the resume predicate still makes sense
* whether the continuation remains relevant
* whether the hold indicates local plateau, drift, or misbinding
* whether a different local composition subset should be active

So reflection is not external to Hold On. It is one of the mechanisms that keeps Hold On semantically sane.

---

## 18. Relation to contemplation

Under the definition in Section 14, contemplation may move up the ladder.

This means contemplation may use a Hold On as evidence that the current lower framing is malformed, too narrow, or wrongly decomposed.

Thus:

[
Contemplate(H) \to
\begin{cases}
decompose\ further,\
maintain\ hold,\
replace\ hold,\
move\ upward\ to\ better\ higher\text{-}order\ Not
\end{cases}
]

This is important because a Hold On is not merely a pause. It is also a structural signal about the adequacy of the current achievedness framing.

---

## 19. Relation to BSSC

Because BSSC is central, Hold On must be expressible as carrier theory, not only scheduler theory.

Let:

[
D^*_{hold}
]

be the carrier of distributed suspension,

and:

[
R^*_{resume}
]

be the carrier of resumed continuation.

Then the canonical movement is:

[
Not\langle X\rangle
\to
D^**{hold}
\to
R^**{resume}
\to
Continue(Not\langle X\rangle)
]

This gives Hold On a BSSC-consistent interpretation:

* suspension is carried
* continuation is carried
* resumption is carried
* the same semantic thread survives distributed interruption

This is the right way to integrate Hold On with the rest of the theory.

---

## 20. Relation to products

Products are side effects of performed duties.

Therefore Hold On is **not** primarily about preserving product generation.

It is about preserving unfinished duty performance over unresolved semantic objects.

If a product emerges after resumption, that product is still a side effect of the resumed achievedness path.

So:

[
HoldOn \to Resume \to DutyPerformance \to Product^{side\ effect}
]

This keeps product secondary, as intended.

## 21. Call families as induced forms

From Hold On theory, a family of call forms falls out naturally.

### 21.1 Request/response call

[
Call_{req/resp}\langle target,\ payload,\ correlation,\ timeout\rangle
]

This induces a message-style Hold On.

### 21.2 Deferred call

[
Call_{deferred}\langle work_id,\ accept,\ completion_channel\rangle
]

This induces a completion-style Hold On.

### 21.3 Publish/listen call

[
Call_{pubsub}\langle topic,\ event,\ listener_predicate\rangle
]

This induces a signal-style Hold On.

### 21.4 Desired/reported convergence call

[
Call_{desired/reported}\langle desired,\ reported,\ converge\rangle
]

This induces a state-convergence Hold On.

### 21.5 Query call

[
Call_{query}\langle subject,\ projection\rangle
]

This normally does **not** induce Hold On, because it does not suspend continuation.

### 21.6 Update call

[
Call_{update}\langle target,\ mutation,\ acceptance,\ completion\rangle
]

This may induce completion-style or approval-style Hold On depending on semantics.

## 22. What Hold On is not allowed to collapse into

Hold On theory forbids several collapses.

### 22.1 No collapse into transport state

A Hold On is not merely a network/socket/topic status.

### 22.2 No collapse into tool runtime

A Hold On is not merely “a tool is still running.”

### 22.3 No collapse into UI pause

A Hold On is not merely “the user said wait.”

### 22.4 No collapse into workflow timer

A timer may be one kind of Hold On, but not the whole theory.

### 22.5 No collapse into callback handling

A callback may trigger resumption, but does not define the semantics of preserved unfinishedness.

## 23. Minimal ledger interpretation

Even though this is theory, it suggests a ledger shape.

A Hold On object should be able to answer at least:

* what unresolved semantic object is being held?
* under what duty?
* what continuation is preserved?
* what resume predicate applies?
* what expiry/failure policy applies?
* what correlation structure applies?
* what multiplicity applies?
* what scope applies?
* what provenance/governance applies?

That is enough to preserve theory-to-implementation coherence.

## 24. Metaphysical summary

BSSC formalizes how achievedness-bearing structures are carried across boundaries.

Hold On formalizes how **unfinished achievedness remains alive across distributed interruption** until valid continuation or reframing becomes possible.

Or in one line:

[
HoldOn = \text{the theory of preserved unfinishedness under distributed continuation}
]

## 25. Compressed formal definition

A compact definition is:

[
HoldOn\langle n,c,r,f,\tau,\kappa,\mu,\sigma,\pi\rangle
]

is the governed, durable suspension of a continuation (c) over unresolved semantic object (n), usually (Not\langle X\rangle), such that the same semantic thread may later resume under explicit resumption condition (r), or else expire, fail, escalate, or reframe under (f), while preserving identity, scope, multiplicity, provenance, and distributed continuity.
